## Problem

`bin/gen-playbook-blocks.sh` warns (non-fatally) when a generated role block exceeds `LINE_WARN_THRESHOLD=40` lines. Two blocks are over it: `templates/prompt-blocks/playbook-pm-spec.md` at 51 lines (49 bullets) and `templates/prompt-blocks/playbook-engineer.md` at 41 lines (39 bullets). The other two — `playbook-qa-verifier.md` (28 lines / 26 bullets) and `playbook-tech-lead.md` (23 lines / 21 bullets) — sit under it. *(All four counts: `pm-spec-measured-by-reading`; the pm-spec and engineer bullet counts were independently corroborated against the committed block files by enumerating the corpus's own `Scope: loop` × `Applies-to` fields and arriving at 49 and 39.)*

The threshold exists because these blocks are spliced verbatim into `agents/*.md` and are therefore paid for on every invocation of the role. The warning's own remedy text says "consider superseding stale entries" — but the corpus has no mechanism for deciding *which* entries are stale, and `bin/check-playbook.sh` deliberately refuses to guess: an entry leaves a shipped block only by being marked `Status: superseded` with a `Superseded-by` pointer at an `active` entry that now covers its ground. That judgment is a content judgment, and this task is the first time it is being made at scale.

## Goal

A human-ratified set of retirements — each one a `(retiree, successor)` pair that satisfies a stated three-condition predicate and every machine constraint `bin/check-playbook.sh` enforces — is applied to `.shell-team/lessons.md` append-preservingly (one `Status` line rewritten and one `Superseded-by` line added per retiree, nothing else touched), the four generated blocks and their four `agents/*.md` consumer regions are regenerated as genuine script output, and the resulting per-block line counts are frozen. Where the ratified set leaves a block above the 40-line threshold, that remaining overage is **explicitly accepted on the board with a stated reason** rather than closed by manufacturing pairs.

### Settled decisions

Each decision below is resolved. Nothing here is left to implementation judgment.

- **D1 — the retirement predicate.** "The older entry's ground is now covered by a newer entry" means all three of the following hold. They are separate tests and each is answered separately, with quoted evidence from both entries' actual text, in the adjudication companion:
  - **(i) Rule subsumption**, in two parts, both required:
    - **(i-a) occasion subsumption** — whenever the older entry's rule fires, the newer entry's rule also fires. The newer entry's occasions may be broader; they may not be a strict special case of the older's.
    - **(i-b) protection subsumption** — at those occasions, the *protection the older entry secures* is still secured by the newer entry's required action. This is an outcome test, not a wording test: the newer entry need not restate every step the older listed, but the failure the older prevents must still be prevented. A step of the older that is merely one *means* to a protection the newer secures by another means does not block retirement; a protection the newer secures by no means at all does.
  - **(ii) Applies-to role coverage does not shrink** — the successor's `Applies-to` role set (with `all` expanded to the four IN roles) must be a superset of the retiree's. **If it would shrink, the verdict is keep, not retire.** A shrinking retirement silently removes a rule from a named role's block with nothing replacing it there, which is the exact harm `bin/check-playbook.sh`'s DP-b directional rule exists to prevent one level up.
  - **(iii) the `Why` incident class is the same** — both entries record the same *kind* of incident, not merely two incidents in the same category. Two entries whose `Why` sections describe structurally different failures do not supersede each other however similar their rules read.
- **D2 — machine constraints are pre-filters, applied before the predicate.** These are properties of `bin/check-playbook.sh` (read, not assumed — see its pass-2 reference-resolution loop and T-1007 DP-b), and a candidate that violates one is out of candidacy regardless of how well it scores on D1:
  - **C1 — an entry that is already the target of another entry's `Superseded-by` cannot be retired** (the checker rejects a chain into a non-`active` target). Two entries are excluded from candidacy on this ground today: `2026-06-12 — Review gate caught a regression in practice (cross-provider review vindicated)` and `2026-07-26 — A change to a completion gate's condition count needs a contamination check and a sentinel-distinctness AC (supersedes two earlier entries)`.
  - **C2 — a `Scope: loop` entry may not point at a `Scope: maintainer` entry** (T-1007 DP-b; the other three direction combinations are legal).
  - **C3 — the `Superseded-by` value must equal the target's `date — title` key exactly after trim** — equality, never containment, em-dash separator included.
  - **C4 — the target's `Status` must be `active`.**
  - **C5 — a `Scope: maintainer` entry never appears in a generated block**, so retiring one is corpus hygiene with **zero** effect on any block's line count and never counts toward the threshold goal.
  - **C6 — retiring an `Applies-to: all` entry removes one bullet from all four blocks at once**, including the two that are already under the threshold.
- **D3 — anti-count-gaming pre-commitment, set before the proposal was written, not after it fell short.** The target arithmetic (−11 bullets for `playbook-pm-spec.md`, −1 for `playbook-engineer.md`) is a *consequence* of the retirement set, never an input to it. If the set of pairs that satisfy D1 and D2 falls short of that arithmetic, **no pair is manufactured, no predicate condition is relaxed, and no borderline pair is upgraded to reach a number.** The task instead lands on issue #97 task 4's branch: the remaining overage is explicitly accepted, on the board, naming each block still over the threshold, its measured line count, and the reason. AC9 is the machine form of that commitment.
- **D4 — the recording venue for retirements is the board plus this task's adjudication companion; `docs/loop-engineering/lessons-import-disposition.md` is not touched.** That ledger is import-scoped by its own opening sentence ("the disposition of every entry considered for **import** into ...") and declares "There are two independent tables". A retirement is not an import disposition, and adding a third table would require rewriting the ledger's own scope statement to hold a record the board and `.shell-team/specs/T-1020-supersede-adjudication.md` already hold durably and citably. **Decided explicitly, not silently mixed:** board + companion, ledger unchanged. AC14 locks the ledger byte-identical.
- **D5 — the corpus edit is exactly two line-level operations per retiree, and nothing else.** For each ratified retiree: the single line `- **Status**: active` becomes `- **Status**: superseded`, and one new line `- **Superseded-by**: <successor key>` is added inside that entry. No prose is rewritten, no entry is deleted, no entry is added, no field is reordered, no `Applies-to` or `Scope` value is edited. In particular, a candidate blocked by D1 (ii) is **not** rescued by widening the successor's `Applies-to` — that is a prose edit, and it is forbidden here.
- **D6 — the generated artifacts must be proven to be genuine script output, not hand-edited.** Regeneration is `bin/gen-playbook-blocks.sh`, and the proof is the scratch-copy regeneration diff CI already runs as its "Dogfood gen-playbook-blocks" step, reproduced locally over all eight registered files (four `templates/prompt-blocks/playbook-*.md` plus the four `agents/*.md` marker regions listed in `templates/prompt-blocks/registry.txt`). AC7.
- **D7 — the quoting rule for an entry key inside a `check:` line.** Entry keys in this corpus contain apostrophes **and** backticks. In every `check:` line, an entry key is written inside **single** quotes, with an embedded apostrophe rendered as the four-character sequence `'\''`. A key is never placed inside double quotes, because a key containing a backtick would then be a command substitution inside our own verification command — the precise class of self-inflicted vacuous pass the corpus's verification-command rule exists to prevent.

## Non-goals

- **Any new lesson.** No entry is added to the corpus by this task. AC6 locks the canonical-entry-heading count against the base ref.
- **Any threshold change.** `LINE_WARN_THRESHOLD=40` in `bin/gen-playbook-blocks.sh` stays exactly as it is. Moving the threshold to meet the corpus is the inverse of the task. AC14.
- **Any `bin/` change, any new test suite, any `.github/**` change.** No checker is written or modified; no CI step is added, removed or reordered. AC14.
- **Targeting the two under-threshold blocks.** No retiree is selected because it would shrink `playbook-qa-verifier.md` or `playbook-tech-lead.md`. Those two blocks may shrink only as a *consequence* of retiring an entry whose `Applies-to` also covers pm-spec or engineer (or is `all`, C6). AC15.
- **Rewriting, re-scoping or re-roling any entry's prose** (D5). Including the tempting case: a candidate blocked by D1 (ii) is kept, not rescued by editing the successor's `Applies-to`.
- **Editing `docs/loop-engineering/lessons-import-disposition.md`** (D4).
- **A CHANGELOG entry, a version bump, or any adopter-facing document change** (`README*`, `docs/`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `templates/todo-template.md`). The only `templates/` files that change are the four generated `playbook-*.md` blocks.
- **Retiring an entry because a tool now enforces it.** Two entries in the corpus describe disciplines a shipped checker now also enforces (a board heading-identity check; a board line-format check). The schema has no "obsolete by mechanization" retirement path — `Status: superseded` structurally requires a successor *entry* — and inventing one would be a `bin/` change. Recorded as an observation in the companion, not acted on.

**Reachable input classes** — what the corpus and its consumers really contain, and what the change must handle correctly:

- **The 83 committed entries of `.shell-team/lessons.md`** as they stand at the base ref: 80 `active` (71 `Scope: loop`, 9 `Scope: maintainer`) and 3 already `superseded`. *(`pm-spec-measured-by-reading`; the derived per-role bullet counts reproduce the four committed blocks exactly, which is the corroboration.)*
- **Entry keys containing shell-significant characters.** Real keys carry apostrophes (`gate's`), backticks (`` `bin/` ``), parentheses, colons, semicolons and the em-dash separator. A `Superseded-by` value and every `check:` line that names a key must reproduce those bytes exactly (C3, D7).
- **Every `Applies-to` shape the schema permits**: a single role, a comma-and-space list, a comma-without-space list (`engineer,qa-verifier` occurs), and the `all` token — all of which `bin/gen-playbook-blocks.sh` trims before matching, so the block membership arithmetic must be derived from the trimmed tokens, not from the raw string.
- **Both `Scope` values**, with the C5 consequence that a maintainer retirement is invisible to every block, and the C2 consequence that a `loop` entry's successor must also be `loop`.
- **The four generated blocks and the four `agents/*.md` marker regions** registered in `templates/prompt-blocks/registry.txt`, each of which must come out of the generator byte-identically (D6).
- **The board as it is**, 500+ KB with one `## Active` section and a long `## Done` section, where this task's entry is inserted and where the overage acceptance (if any) is recorded.

**Out-of-scope synthetic extremes** — declined deliberately; a finding grounded only in one of these is out of contract:

- **Malformed corpus inputs**: a NUL byte, an unterminated or ambiguous fence, a forged or non-canonical `## ` heading, a multi-line field value, a control character in a field, an unknown field bullet, a duplicate entry key. `bin/check-playbook.sh` already rejects each of these fail-closed and this task changes no checker; AC3 confirms the corpus passes, it does not re-derive the validator's own coverage.
- **Adversarial keys constructed to defeat equality matching** — a key that is a prefix or substring of another, a key differing only by trailing whitespace or by a hyphen where the em-dash belongs. Equality-not-containment is T-108's decision, tested in `tests/gen-playbook-blocks/` and `bin/check-playbook.sh`'s own suite; escalating exotic near-miss keys here is not an open finding class.
- **Hypothetical or future corpus entries.** The adjudication is over the entries committed at the base ref. "But what if a later entry made this pair valid" is not a finding.
- **Corpus files other than the one `bin/team-paths.sh --get lessons` resolves to**, and multi-file `check-playbook.sh` invocations.
- **CRLF line endings, a missing final newline, and non-UTF-8 bytes** anywhere in the corpus or the consumers. The generator's `head`/`tail` splice already preserves them outside the marker region (T-047), and no such shape exists in the committed files.
- **Scale**: a corpus of thousands of entries, a single entry of megabyte length, or a block whose regeneration time matters.
- **The retirement predicate applied to anything other than the committed corpus** — for example to a candidate raised in a retro but never promoted. Those are the import ledger's subject (D4), not this task's.

**Order of operations.** Edit the corpus first, then run `bin/gen-playbook-blocks.sh` from the repository root — never hand-edit a block or a marker region, because AC7 regenerates into a scratch copy and `cmp`s all eight files. Note that the generator refuses to write anything at all if `bin/check-playbook.sh` rejects the corpus (its fail-closed preflight), so a malformed Superseded-by value surfaces as "nothing was regenerated", not as a bad block.

**Gotchas.**

- The generator's own threshold warning goes to **stderr** and does not fail the run. Do not read a clean exit as "the block is under the threshold" — AC8 and AC9 are the measurements.
- `wc -l` prints leading whitespace on this platform; every check pipes it through `tr -d ' '`.
- A retirement produces **one** deletion and **two** additions per entry in the corpus diff (the Status line is a rewrite). AC5's expected numbers are N and 2N, not 0 and N — and the deletion column is the right instrument precisely because the corpus is an all-bullet file where a deleted line begins with two hyphens.
- `agents/pm-spec.md`'s own playbook region is one of the eight regenerated files, so this task edits the prompt of the role that wrote this spec. That is expected; it is also why AC7's byte comparison, not a visual read, is the acceptance.
- **AC9's anchor phrase is a self-reference hazard on the board.** AC9 greps the board for the frozen phrase `T-1020 overage accepted`. This task's own board entry discusses that mechanism in prose, and if the prose ever quotes the anchor verbatim, AC9 passes on a *description* of an acceptance rather than an acceptance. The entry as written at task-open deliberately paraphrases it ("accept the remaining overage under D3") for exactly this reason. Write the anchor exactly once, on the real acceptance line, and only when the acceptance is real — and re-read the entry after editing to confirm there is still exactly one occurrence.

**Prior art.** `bin/check-playbook.sh`'s pass-2 reference-resolution loop (AC1–AC7 of T-108, plus T-1007 DP-b) is the authority on what a legal retirement looks like; the three retirements already in the corpus — one from 2026-06-13 and the two folded into the 2026-07-26 completion-gate entry — are the worked examples, and the 2026-07-26 entry's title (`...
