**Goal (one sentence).** In STOP mode, and only when the orchestrator supplies the three convergence inputs, `bin/rework-digest.sh` prints — immediately after its `stop-reason:` line — a `convergence:` line whose value is exactly one of `converging` and `not-converging` and never a third value, followed by three indented ground lines (`trend:`, `never-dropped:`, `instances:`) carrying the closed-vocabulary evidence the verdict was derived from and a fourth indented `convergence-action:` line naming which action leads; the verdict is derived as `converging` if and only if the per-round finding trend is `falling`, no never-dropped component is in state `hit-this-round`, and every class label repeating in the records is declared a `distinct` instance; supplying none of the three inputs leaves the digest's stdout byte-identical to the base-ref script's for every argument shape that exists today, supplying some but not all of them exits 2 with nothing on stdout and one usage block on stderr, and the assembled digest — including every caller-supplied component name — still passes the script's own `goal-state.sh signature` self-check or is refused by it with a message naming the offending field; and both `skills/run/SKILL.md` and `skills/goal/SKILL.md` carry one canonical, registry-mirrored instruction to open the escalation message with that verdict in the operator's language, then its three grounds in that language, then the digest's raw stdout verbatim.

**The verdict states; it never decides.** Nothing in this task gates, suppresses, delays or short-circuits an escalation, and no new operator gate is created. `bin/loop-guard.sh` keeps printing exactly what it prints today, `budget.max_iterations` is unchanged, and the escalation still reaches the operator whatever the verdict says. A `converging` verdict is a recommendation the operator reads first, not a decision the loop has taken.

**The opt-in seam, and why it is where it is.** The convergence block appears only when at least one of `--rounds-total`, `--never-dropped` and `--instance` is supplied. With none of them, the script's stdout for a given argument list is byte-identical to the branch-point script's stdout for that same list (**AC3**) — which is what keeps T-1095's merged **AC9** green, since that criterion `cmp`s exactly such a pair. With one or two of them, the digest is refused outright rather than printed with a partial verdict (**AC4**): a `convergence:` line whose grounds are incomplete is worse than no line at all, because its whole value to the operator is that the three grounds behind it are all present. The seam is a refusal boundary at the script layer and not a gate on the benefit: the shipped instruction in both skills tells the orchestrator to supply the inputs at every STOP escalation, so on the default path the block is always there.

**Mode scope, and which stop reasons accept the block.** The block is accepted in STOP mode (`--stop-reason`) only; any convergence input paired with `--trigger same-class-2` is refused (**AC4**). Two grounds, either sufficient: a same-class-2 firing happens at the moment a label reaches two occurrences, typically with one or two rounds of history, where the trend this verdict is built on is not yet defined; and T-1095 **AC9**'s byte-pin reads exactly that mode with exactly those arguments. Within STOP mode the block is accepted for **all four** stop reasons, not `max_iterations_reached` alone: the three indicators are computed from rework history that exists identically whichever STOP fired, and every STOP puts the same continue-or-stop question to the operator, so restricting the verdict to one reason would withhold it at three moments where it is equally well-defined and equally wanted.

**How the trend is derived, including the cases a short or tied series produces.** The series is derived from the existing `--round`/`--phase`/`--class` triples: `s[r]` is the number of records carrying round number `r`, and the series is `s[1] … s[n]` where `n` is the caller-supplied `--rounds-total`. A round that ran and produced no classified finding contributes a real zero, which is why `--rounds-total` exists and is mandatory: a round absent from the triples is indistinguishable, from the triples alone, from a round that never happened. The classification is: `falling` when `n` is at least 2 **and** `s[n] < s[1]` **and** `s[n] <= s[n-1]`; `rising` when `n` is at least 2 **and** either `s[n] > s[1]` or `s[n] > s[n-1]`; `flat` in every remaining case, which includes `n = 1` and the tie `s[n] = s[1]` with a non-rising last step. The three are mutually exclusive and exhaustive by construction. Last-versus-first is what makes a real loop's noisy descent read as a descent — T-1134's own measured nine-round QA series, `1 1 1 0 0 0 0 1 0`, is not monotone and is nonetheless the operator's own recorded case of a converging loop — and the added last-versus-previous conjunct is what stops a series that collapsed and then rebounded, such as `5 0 0 0 4`, from reading as `falling` on its endpoints alone.

**The derivation rule, and the two questions it settles that a label count cannot.** `converging` holds if and only if all three of: the trend is `falling`; no `--never-dropped` component is in state `hit-this-round`; and every class label occurring twice or more in the records carries `--instance <class>=distinct`. Otherwise `not-converging`. The second condition admits `hit-earlier` alongside `green` deliberately: a never-dropped component hit once in an earlier round, closed and not recurring, is T-1134's measured shape while that loop converged, and the pre-commitment rule this condition serves is about a component defeated across **consecutive** rounds, which is exactly what a `hit-this-round` state standing beside an earlier one expresses. The third condition is vacuously satisfied when no label repeats. It follows — and is settled here rather than left to a reader — that distinct instances under one repeated label with a **non-falling** count read `not-converging`, because the conjunction requires a falling trend: distinctness can only remove a false `not-converging` that a bare label count would have produced, never manufacture a `converging` on its own.

**What the verdict changes about the recommended action, and what it deliberately leaves alone.** The block's own `convergence-action:` line names which action leads — `extend` under `converging`, `reconsider-design-premise` under `not-converging` — and it is printed near the top of the digest, above the rounds list and above the existing `recommended-action:` block. The existing `note:`, `judgment:`, `repeated-classes:` and `recommended-action:` lines, including the `(a)`/`(b)`/`(c)` choices, are **not rewritten, not reordered and not made conditional on any new input**: no existing digest line's bytes vary with a convergence input, which is what keeps the entire merged fixture set and T-1095 **AC9** and **AC10** green by construction rather than by a matrix of new comparisons. Where a `converging` verdict stands beside a `same-class-repetition` judgment — the T-1134 and T-1143 case — the two are not in conflict and the skills' shipped instruction says so in the operator's language: the label repeated, its instances were distinct, the trend is falling, so `convergence-action:` leads and the `recommended-action:` menu below it is the fallback. ## Input space

**Reachable input classes** — the situations this verdict, this parser and this instruction really meet, which the implementation must handle correctly:

1. A STOP escalation carrying between one and roughly a dozen rounds of classified findings, with some rounds carrying none — the measured shape of T-1134, whose nine QA rounds produced findings in four of them and whose five review rounds produced none in the last.
2. A per-round series that is not monotone: it falls, sits at zero for several rounds, rises once, and falls again. T-1134's `1 1 1 0 0 0 0 1 0` is the recorded instance.
3. A series of exactly one round, and a series of exactly two rounds with equal counts.
4. A series that collapses to zero and rebounds on the final round.
5. A class label occurring two or more times across rounds whose occurrences are **distinct** instances — T-1134's four-round label, and T-1143's three-round one.
6. A class label occurring two or more times whose occurrences are the **same** instance recurring.
7. Records in which no class label repeats at all, so the instance question is vacuous.
8. A never-dropped set of one to roughly half a dozen components, each in any of the three states, including a set mixing a closed earlier hit with otherwise-green components.
9. A never-dropped component name that satisfies the slug charset yet puts a whole-word `goal-state.sh signature` token into the assembled digest — `live-pass-gate`, `qa-fail-mode`, `ac10-parser` are the real spellings this class produces.
10. An orchestrator supplying none of the three inputs: every invocation shaped as it was before this task, including every `--trigger same-class-2` invocation.
11. An orchestrator supplying some but not all of them — forgetting `--rounds-total`, or omitting `--instance` for a class it did not notice had repeated.
12. All four `--stop-reason` values, each with and without the convergence inputs.
13. A `--rounds-total` smaller than the highest `--round` in the records — an ordinary miscount by the orchestrator, not an adversarial input.

**Out-of-scope synthetic extremes** — inputs this spec explicitly declines to protect:

- A `--rounds-total` above the declared three-digit bound, or a series long enough to raise a memory or runtime question. The bound is the protection: the loop contract's own `budget.max_iterations` is 3, the largest value any record in this repository carries is 9, and a three-digit ceiling leaves two orders of magnitude of headroom while making the series length bounded by construction. Values above it are refused (**AC4**), not handled.
- A never-dropped list of dozens or hundreds of components. Nothing refuses one, and nothing is claimed about the readability or the line length of the resulting ground line.
- Component names or class slugs carrying non-ASCII bytes, control characters or an exotic text encoding. The slug charset refuses them and no criterion here protects any encoding behaviour beyond that refusal.
- A name constructed to defeat `goal-state.sh signature`'s regex through a locale difference. That checker sets `LC_ALL=C` inside its own `signature` branch; no criterion here re-verifies its regex engine.
- Instruction prose in either skill that quotes the convergence requirement inside a framing clause rejecting it. That class is open-ended and is deliberately assigned to the cross-provider review's semantic-assent read rather than to a criterion, on the split **AC12** states.
- An orchestrator that supplies well-formed inputs carrying **false** values — declaring a component green that was hit, or a recurrence distinct. The script records what it is told, exactly as it already does for `--class` and `--reflection`; detecting a mis-declared ground is outside every criterion here and outside this script's design.
