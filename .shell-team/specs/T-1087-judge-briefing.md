## Problem

`docs/loop-engineering/phase-multiplexing.md`'s `## Tier-2 judge design and cost comparison` section fixes a judge contract on paper — four inputs, four ranked criteria, five output fields, three forbiddens, three retained bounds — and stops there, deliberately: it declares `- verification-class: no-mechanism` and builds nothing. Issue #307's precondition 2 and issue #308 item 1 name what that leaves missing before a Tier-2 arm can actually fire. A judge instance handed those contract lines still cannot produce a deterministic, auditable selection, because three things the contract never states are exactly the three things a selection needs: **what values a criterion's score can take**, **how two score vectors are compared and what breaks a borderline**, and **when `none` is the right answer rather than the least-bad tree**. A scale with no anchors is not a procedure; two judges given "0–3" diverge, and a divergent instrument cannot measure `tier2-rework-rounds-avoided`, which is the only parameter that would move the tier off `not-yet`.

Three further obligations sit outside #308's own list and would be silently dropped if the briefing were assembled from that list alone: the subject brief's four `- candidate-rule:` lines are explicitly labelled "a later judge checks these, never this subject's own oracle" and belong to no criterion until someone maps them; the harness note's `- oracle-claim:` and its three `- claim-limit:` lines exist precisely so a judge reading `check-results` knows the strength of the instrument it is reading, and an `11 passed, 0 failed` is not conformance; and for this subject the `merge` disposition is structurally unreachable, so a judge that takes that branch is reasoning about an option it cannot legally exercise.

T-1087 ships the briefing that closes all of this, as one committed document whose single verbatim-handoff region a T-1088 orchestrator hands unedited to a context-free judge instance.
