## Re-freeze record

**v1→v2 — class B — intent-preserving repair of defective frozen bytes.** Ratified by the codex round-1 review finding (`.shell-team/reviews/T-1050.md`, two Majors, both against `R186` only) together with this spec's own already-frozen #186 intent, which the repair restores rather than changes. The scope of the review's veto was itself scoped: the T-1040, T-1037 and T-1027 re-freezes and the #182 and #189 items carry no finding and are untouched by this delta.

**What was wrong.** `R186` v1 recommended `git ls-files -z -- '<pathspec>' | xargs -0 grep -n -- '<pattern>'` as a tracked-file enumeration and claimed that a tracked-file enumeration "cannot reach an untracked or gitignored file at all". Both halves were defective, and — measured by the reviewer through direct construction, not argued — in the two ways a rule governing *absence* checks can least afford:

1. **The exit contract cannot express absence.** `xargs` aggregates its children's statuses. A population split into batches returns the same non-zero status whether one batch matched the forbidden pattern, one batch failed to read, or both; negating the aggregate — the shape a negated absence assertion needs — reports absence while the pattern genuinely occurs. Reproduced by forcing a one-file-per-batch split with one matching and one non-matching file.
2. **The reading target leaves the tracked set.** `git ls-files` lists a tracked symlink's own path, and the external `grep` that `xargs` invokes follows the link into whatever it points at, which can be untracked or gitignored. So the sentence's own stated rationale was true of `git grep` and false of the pipeline it was attached to. Confirmed by a constructed symlink test.

**What changed, and why it closes the class rather than the instances.** v2 keeps `git grep` (and names `--cached`) as the only recommended forms, and states the **two properties** that earn them the position — one command with one exit status, so a completed-and-matched-nothing read is distinguishable from a read that did not complete; and a reading target that cannot leave the tracked set — followed by the instruction to *judge any form you reach for by those two properties rather than by whether it appears in this list*. A reader meeting a form nobody enumerated now has a test rather than a list. The `git ls-files … | xargs … grep` pipeline is **named and rejected in the shipped text** instead of quietly dropped, with both failure modes stated, because that pipeline was issue #186's own suggested wording: omitting it would leave the next author to reinvent it from the issue. The factual claim about untracked and gitignored content is re-attached to the two forms it is actually true of.
