## Problem

`bin/check-handoff.sh` is the loop's most-run checker — every phase gate invokes it at least once, `bin/close-out.sh` invokes it twice per close-out, and CI runs it against this repository's real board on every push and pull request. Issue #269 reports it taking roughly two minutes against this repository's board. The board is an append-only record: closed entries accumulate in `## Done` and never leave, and each live entry's continuation narrative grows through the task's own rounds. A checker whose cost tracks either of those growth curves gets slower every cycle for reasons that have nothing to do with what it is checking, and the loop pays that toll once per phase.

The cost is real and already recorded twice inside this repository. `.shell-team/todo.md`'s own T-1068 entry carries a QA round-1 finding that measured the identical command at 14.95 s / 14.92 s / 14.99 s under the shell's default UTF-8 locale against a 2,802,713-byte board, and 2.08 s under `LC_ALL=C` on the same bytes; it isolated a single 5,008-byte continuation line as costing roughly 12.3 s of that total on its own, and named `bin/check-handoff.sh:136`'s per-line blank test `[[ -z "${content//[[:space:]]/}" ]]` as the suspected quadratic. The same entry records the board reaching ≈17.96–17.98 s and then ≈21.73–21.77 s within the same task as the board grew, and cross-references a third data point (≈33 s, issue #256). None of those measurements was taken against the board state issue #269 reports, and none separates the checker's two candidate cost paths from each other. What exists is a well-evidenced symptom with an unconfirmed cause.

## Summarized sources

Five distinctions are carried over. **(i)** The script has exactly **two** whole-input passes: an `awk` extraction at lines 53–57 that reads **every line of the file** (it clears `in_active` at the next `^## ` heading but never stops reading), and a `while read` loop at lines 127–177 that iterates **only the extracted `## Active` lines**. Any cost model must attribute time to one of those two, and they grow along different axes. **(ii)** The observable contract is `<file>:<lineno>: <reason>: <line>` on stderr with nothing on stdout, exit `2` for an unreadable input (line 28), exit `1` when any violation was emitted (line 179), exit `0` otherwise; `NR` inside the awk is what makes the reported line number the **original file's**, so any change to how the file is traversed can silently renumber every message. **(iii)** Three grammar constants — `LINE_RE` (line 75), `TASK_LINE_RE` (117), `CONTINUATION_RE` (119) — plus the seven-entry `ALLOWED_FLAGS` array (32–40) and `flag_allowed()` are the whole vocabulary; this task changes none of them. **(iv)** The awk's `!seen` guard means only the **first** `## Active` heading opens the section; a second one later in the file opens nothing. **(v)** The blank test at line 136, the CR strip at 134 and the two `${raw%%…}` / `${raw#*…}` splits at 129–130 are per-Active-line bash string operations whose cost is a function of that line's own length, not of the board's size.

**Goal (one sentence).** `bin/check-handoff.sh` is profiled first — its whole-file `awk` extraction and its `## Active`-only bash loop measured separately, under stated conditions, with one dominant term named from that measurement rather than from any prior attribution — and is then changed so that its runtime stops tracking the board's monotonic growth, while every byte of its observable verdict (exit code, stderr message format, reason strings, reported line numbers, output order) is proven unchanged against the pre-change implementation over a fixed differential corpus.

**Diagnosis before repair.** The dominant cost term is **not settled** by this spec and must not be assumed by the implementation. Two candidates exist, they grow along different axes, and the evidence available at authoring time is consistent with either:

- the **`done` axis** — total board size. The `awk` extraction reads every line of the file, so its cost grows with `## Done`, which grows monotonically and is never validated.
- the **`active` axis** — the byte length of the lines inside `## Active`. The per-line bash string operations cost a function of each line's own length; a task's own hand-off narrative grows through its rounds and shrinks back to zero for that task at close-out, when its entry moves to `## Done`.

Issue #256's quadratic-blank-test attribution is a candidate on the `active` axis, recorded as **relayed and unconfirmed**. The measured facts that keep it open in both directions: this repository's board recorded ≈15 s while its `## Active` held one task's long hand-off entry, and the figure relayed for a later, larger board is ≈122 s — but that later board's `## Active`, measured immediately **before this task's own entry was appended to it**, spanned eight lines and held **no task entry at all**, which the `active`-axis hypothesis alone does not explain. That measurement is pinned to an event rather than stated in the present tense on purpose: a board is a live artifact, this task's own entry changes its `## Active`, and a present-tense claim about it would be stale in the same commit that froze it. The task therefore begins with a profile that separates the two paths numerically and names one dominant term; the repair follows the profile, and the profile's verdict — including a verdict that contradicts #256 — is recorded as a finding rather than smoothed over.

**What "stops tracking growth" means, stated as an outcome rather than an implementation.** Runtime measured on the `done` axis must be effectively invariant to `## Done` growth: quadrupling the board's `## Done` content, with `## Active` held constant, must not double the runtime. On the `active` axis, if the profile names it dominant, the same must hold for a quadrupling of `## Active`'s byte content; if it does not, that axis carries a no-regression floor instead. Constant-factor shaving does not satisfy this and is not what the task is for. How the outcome is reached is the engineer's call, informed by the profile.

**What must not move.** The checker's observable verdict is frozen. For every input in a fixed differential corpus, the pre-change implementation (read from the branch point's committed blob) and the post-change implementation must produce byte-identical stdout, byte-identical stderr and the same exit status — including exit `2` for an unreadable or missing input, which `bin/close-out.sh:378` discriminates from `1` at its fail-closed floor, and including the two source lines `tests/errexit-safe/run.sh` pins by exact content with a declared count of one each. The three grammar constants and the flag vocabulary are byte-identical to the branch point. The line numbers in every message stay the original file's.

**What the task leaves behind.** One measurement record at `docs/loop-engineering/check-handoff-scaling.md` carrying the measurement protocol (both locales, both bash versions, the board's identity read as a committed blob, its size, the `## Active` span, the trial count), the separated profile with one named dominant term, the before/after scaling curves on both axes, the verdict-preservation result, and a consumer inventory produced as a ref-pinned extraction command with its output pasted verbatim. Six section-structure fixtures join `tests/check-handoff/fixtures/` and the existing suite exercises them. `bin/check-board-headings.sh`'s stale line reference into this file is repaired to resolve to the construct it claims to mirror.

## Non-goals

- **No change to the status-flag vocabulary.** The `ALLOWED_FLAGS` array and `flag_allowed()` are byte-identical to the branch point's blob. No flag is added, removed or renamed.

- **No `## Done` archiving, splitting, rotation or truncation of any board.** The board's shape is not this task's to change; the checker is made cheap on the board that exists, not the board made small for the checker. No board content is deleted or moved by this task beyond appending its own entry.

- **No CI wiring change.** `.github/workflows/check-handoff.yml` is byte-identical to the branch point: the script and the suite are already wired, and the new fixtures are data the existing suite step picks up.
- **No new runtime dependency and no portability regression.** Pure bash valid under bash 3.2 (no `mapfile`/`readarray`, no associative arrays, no `${var^^}`/`${var,,}`) and POSIX awk only (no `gensub`, `asort`, `systime`, `strftime`, `IGNORECASE`, `FIELDWIDTHS`, `patsplit`, `nextfile`).
