## Problem

The loop runs one agent per phase, so a phase's wall clock is whatever that single agent takes even when the phase's work is a list of independent, read-only units that N agents could take a slice of each. T-1062 is the measured case: its engineer phase spent the bulk of its 134 minutes not on the edit but on mechanically-enumerable verification — a full-population blast-radius diff over the spec corpus, a CI-equivalent run, mutation probes — every unit independent and read-only. Issue #253 asks whether fanning that kind of work over N instances of one role actually pays, and names the counter-force as its central research question: the more agents share one task, the more precision the orchestrator and the PM's decomposition must supply, and that precision cost can eat the saving or lengthen the total. Nobody can currently say where the line falls, because the only concurrency figure this repository has measured is a single N=2 point taken as a side observation in another task. This task answers the question for the width axis and commits to no mechanism.

### Verdict scope (operator-ratified)

**Authorship: operator-ratified.** The primary record is the `- intervention: human-correction` entry dated 2026-08-14 in `.shell-team/interventions/T-1066.md`. This section restates that ruling for the gates that read this spec; it is not this spec's self-discipline and it is not re-litigated here.

For this deliverable — an internal feasibility investigation whose purpose is to inform a decision about restructuring how the loop spends machine time — **deliverable correctness means two things**, and a finding against either is verdict-gating: (1) the **computed values** are correct, meaning each figure names the source it was read from and reproduces when the command quoted beside it is re-run; and (2) the **conclusions' direction** is right, meaning what the note says its numbers support is what they support — a tier's disposition, the dividing-line verdict, the `measured`-legitimacy judgment, the aggregation-correctness verdict, the recommendation.

**Connective prose is not verdict-gating.** A finding about a sentence that joins, summarises or characterises values that are themselves correct — a totality word whose domain is wider than its command's, a label mismatch, a stale count in a narrative aside, an imprecise summary — is recorded as a **note** in the review record and does not on its own produce `REQUEST_CHANGES` or `FAIL`, provided every computed value it touches is correct and no conclusion's direction moves. **No escalation ratchet is created over prose classes**: repeated findings of a connective-prose class do not raise a gate's severity, do not create a halt condition, and do not on their own trigger a rework round; they accumulate as notes and are dispositioned at close-out.

This is a statement about what gates the verdict, not permission to be careless. A prose finding is still reported, and the engineer may still fix one — it simply does not stop the task.
