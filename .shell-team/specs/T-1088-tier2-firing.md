## Problem

`docs/loop-engineering/phase-multiplexing.md` prices Tier 2 entirely on paper: it builds a break-even model from six recorded `- cost-input:` figures, solves it for a threshold of **≈0.739 rework rounds at N=2**, and then records the one parameter carrying the tier's actual bet — `- break-even: tier2-rework-rounds-avoided` — as `undetermined`, because no recorded field measures how many rework rounds a judge's pick decisions would actually avoid. Its own recommendation stays `not-yet` on exactly that ground. Two of issue #307's three preconditions have since been discharged as committed bytes — a reusable subject with one measured serial baseline (`docs/loop-engineering/tier2-subject-harness.md`), and a scoring contract instantiated as a verbatim-handoff region a context-free judge instance can act on (`docs/loop-engineering/tier2-judge-briefing.md`) — and neither of them has ever been run.

What is missing is not another model. It is **one observation**: the tier's mechanics executed end to end on a real subject, with two real candidate implementations, a real judge pass over the frozen briefing region, and **real gates** — the precondition #307 names third and the one the harness note's own `- commensurability: rework-round-term — not-supported` line says its baseline cannot substitute for. Until that happens, the adoption question has no measured input at all, and the loop's most expensive open design decision rests on arithmetic over figures borrowed from other tasks.

This task fires the arm exactly once, at N=2, and publishes one note whose adoption-decision material is this firing's own measured rounds-avoided figure and its own measured break-even threshold, each read against the modelled ≈0.739. It measures; it does not adopt.
