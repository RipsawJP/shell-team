## Problem

The loop couples two things that are separable: **who authors the spec** and **whether the loop's machinery runs**. Issue #331 records a field observation of the cost of that coupling. In a hub-and-spoke setup — an orchestrator resident in a coordination repo, implementation in separate repos — the decision inputs for an accuracy-critical change (measured facts across repos, live-environment confirmations, incident history) lived in the orchestrator's own context. Delegating spec *authorship* to `pm-spec` there had arithmetically zero value: writing a complete hand-off package **is** writing the spec, and the delegation converts verified first-hand facts into relayed ones, which is the exact degradation this repository's own relayed-premise discipline exists to fight. `pm-spec` earns its keep when *formalization* is the bottleneck, not when *integrating verified facts* is.

The orchestrator therefore declined the full loop and hand-rolled a hybrid (self-written spec, delegate implement and review only). The hybrid went off the rails, with implementation and review diverging from intent at high correction cost. The refusal was aimed at `pm-spec` authorship, but because authorship is coupled to the machinery, refusing authorship silently discarded the machinery too: the frozen intent block, the board records, the freeze sweep, both gates, the interventions ledger — precisely the mechanisms that catch an orchestrator's own mistakes. This task decouples the two, so an already-authored spec is a supported entry into the full loop rather than a reason to leave it.
