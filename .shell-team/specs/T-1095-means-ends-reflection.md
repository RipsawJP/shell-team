# Self-triggered means-ends reflection at the three escalation firing points: one canonical four-question block mirrored into both skills through the prompt-sync registry, a disposition rule that executes an already-pre-priced drop on the loop's own authority with the three standing human gates unchanged, and an optional `rework-digest.sh` field carrying the four answers

## Problem

The loop already detects, mechanically, all three moments at which a rework is going in circles: the same-class-2 count reaching two, a spec pre-commitment's factual trigger firing, and `bin/loop-guard.sh` printing `STOP:<reason>`. What it does with each of them is the same thing — compose an escalation and hand the decision to the operator. During T-1092 that produced four stops and six operator rulings on one task while a single auxiliary component was patched, redesigned and finally dropped, and the escalations themselves were what kept the sprint from its goal: the pre-commitment already carried a pre-priced drop, yet at each firing the drop was presented as one option beside a cheaper-looking patch. Every individual patch was reviewer-verified and genuinely small; the spiral was invisible step-locally and obvious in aggregate. One reflective prompt run by hand identified it immediately. Nothing in the shipped loop performs that reflection, so the loop cannot escape its own spirals without spending an operator interruption per round — and the escalation-to-human boundary is the one surface where a wrong default is expensive in both directions.

**Goal (one sentence).** At each of the three mechanically detectable firing points — the same-class-2 count reaching two, a spec pre-commitment's factual trigger, and a loop-guard `STOP:<reason>` — the orchestrator answers four fixed questions in writing **before** composing any escalation, and when the answers are (1) every item of the never-dropped set green, (2) every finding in the firing class against auxiliary components, and (3) a pre-priced disposition existing for each of those components, the disposition for the earliest of them in the pre-commitment's recorded drop order **executes on the loop's own authority and is recorded on the board with the four answers as its ground**; escalation to the operator stays mandatory when no pre-priced disposition exists for one of the named components, when the never-dropped set is hit, when the disposition itself is destructive or irreversible, or when the finding set is empty or unclassified — **an empty or unclassified finding set escalates and never executes a disposition**, because conditions (2) and (3) are vacuously true over an empty set and a predicate that cannot evaluate its input must report that rather than pass; the norm is authored **once** as a canonical `contain`-mode prompt block registered against both consuming skills so `bin/check-prompt-sync.sh` enforces the mirror mechanically rather than by hand; `bin/rework-digest.sh` optionally gains a closed-enum `--reflection` field so the digest carries the reflection's outcome; and T-1093's single live firing ships as the named worked example.

**The norm is authored once and mirrored by a checker, not by hand.** The canonical text lives in `templates/prompt-blocks/means-ends-reflection.md` and is registered in `templates/prompt-blocks/registry.txt` in `contain` mode against exactly two consumers, `skills/run/SKILL.md` and `skills/goal/SKILL.md` — the case `registry.txt:12–15` defines that mode for, since each consumer wraps the core sentences in a role-specific bullet whose tail (which step it sits in, which sibling bullets it is adjacent to) legitimately differs. Two limits of that mechanization are stated here rather than discovered later. First, `bin/check-prompt-sync.sh:125–131` tests containment **per file**, so the checker cannot express "the run skill carries this in step 5 *and* in step 6"; the three-region presence is therefore asserted by **AC4**, **AC5** and **AC6**, one criterion per region, and the checker's exit code (**AC3**) is the file-level half only. Second, `registry.txt:25–29`'s own scope limit applies unchanged: a future skill or agent that copies these sentences without being registered is invisible to the checker, and registering a new consumer is part of adding it.

**The disposition rule, and the shape of the authority it exercises.** The rule executes a decision that was already priced, by the same party, before the first review round: a pre-commitment names its droppable components in order at freeze time precisely because one more patch is always the cheapest-looking move at the moment a trigger fires. Executing it is therefore not new authority but the **absence** of a re-negotiation, and the loop's licence for it is the operator's standing loop-escape instruction of 2026-08-23, whose primary in-repo record is `.shell-team/todo.md`'s T-1093 loop-escape record: "Executed on the orchestrator's own authority per the operator's standing loop-escape instruction (2026-08-23); the remaining human gates (batch GO, planning, destructive ops) are untouched." Every report that names an executed disposition satisfies the existing authorship-declaration duty (`skills/run/SKILL.md:80`, `skills/goal/SKILL.md:240`) by naming which of the two authorship classes the cited disposition belongs to — AI self-discipline, self-imposed and never operator-ratified, or an operator-ratified ruling — and a task's own pre-commitment is the first of those two, never the second. The exception set is closed at **four** cases and each of them is an escalation, not a judgment call: no pre-priced disposition exists for one of the components the reflection named; the never-dropped set is hit; the disposition itself is destructive or irreversible; or the finding set is empty or unclassified.

**The empty-finding-set branch, stated rather than left to inference.** Two of the three execution conditions quantify over the findings in the firing class, so both are **vacuously true when that class is empty** — the reachable case being a `STOP:budget_exhausted` or `STOP:guard_error` arriving before any rework round was ever classified. The rule therefore fails closed at exactly that point: **an empty or unclassified finding set escalates and never executes a disposition**, and the same holds for a finding the orchestrator has not yet classified into a root-cause class, because an unclassified finding cannot be shown to target an auxiliary component. This is the repository's own checker discipline applied to a duty rather than to a script — a predicate that cannot evaluate its input reports that instead of passing — and it is written into the canonical block itself (**AC2**) so all three gate surfaces carry it (**AC4**, **AC5**, **AC6**) rather than inheriting it from this paragraph.

**Question (4) is recorded, not gating, and the enum's precedence is fixed.** The execution predicate is conditions (1)–(3); question (4)'s aggregate-cost answer is **recorded for audit and does not gate the execution**, deliberately, because the drop was priced at freeze time and re-litigating it on cost at the firing moment is exactly the re-negotiation this norm exists to remove. Stated so a later reader does not read the omission as an oversight. Relatedly, where more than one escalation condition holds at once, the single recorded audit value is the earliest in this fixed order: `escalated-primary-not-green`, `escalated-never-dropped`, `escalated-irreversible`, `escalated-no-disposition`. Precedence settles only which label is recorded — every one of those branches escalates either way, so the safety property does not depend on it.

**What bounds a standing rule grounded in one live firing.** The evidence is n=1 (T-1093), and two things bound it. First, structurally: the four-case exception set above gates **every** firing regardless of how many have occurred, and every execution is recorded on the board, so the failure mode of a wrong generalization is visible in the record rather than silent. Second, by a stated **re-evaluation trigger**: **while at most three live firings are recorded** — the retro following the **third** firing itself included, deliberately, because that retro is the confirming read the threshold's own rationale exists to obtain, and a bound reading "fewer than three" would graduate the norm to permanent, unreviewed standing at exactly the firing whose review was supposed to confirm it — this norm's standing is re-read at each retro that follows a cycle in which it fired, with the board's own loop-escape records as the ledger, and a re-read finding a firing whose shape departs from T-1093's returns the norm to planning rather than amending it in place. Three is the threshold because this repository already treats the second instance of a class as establishing it and further instances as confirmation (issue #346's own measured failure shape says exactly that of T-1092's six rounds), so three recorded firings is the smallest count that is established **and** confirmed rather than observed once. This trigger is a **re-read duty on an existing gate, not a new one**: it consumes the retro that already happens, adds no status flag, no checker and no stop, and the three standing human gates are unchanged.

**Why the norm text is written in the plural, measured rather than assumed.** The four questions as drafted in issue #346 carry singular determiners — "*the* task's primary value", "*the* auxiliary component", "*a* pre-priced disposition". Two specs in this repository's own corpus already carry **two** ordered droppable components each (`.shell-team/specs/T-1093-verification-ceiling.md:50`, "Droppable 1st" and "Droppable 2nd"; `docs/loop-engineering/tier2-subject-harness.md:106–112`, the second rep first and the regeneration driver second), and T-1093's never-dropped set likewise holds two items. A norm assuming cardinality one would therefore have shipped carrying the class it exists to close. The shipped text consequently asks which components the findings target, whether a pre-priced disposition exists for **each** of them, and what **drop order** the pre-commitment recorded; the rule then executes the disposition for the **earliest** such component in that order, one firing at a time.

**Pre-commitment (AI self-discipline — self-imposed by this loop at plan and freeze time, never operator-ratified; any report citing it says so).** Factual trigger: two consecutive review rounds landing an independent Blocker or Major against the **same component**. Contextual condition: before a third round on that component starts; the factual trigger governs when the two disagree. **At the trigger the drop is the DEFAULT, executed on the orchestrator's own authority, not an option presented for negotiation** — this task's own subject matter applied to itself. **Droppable 1st**: the `bin/rework-digest.sh --reflection` field together with its fixture and its golden-lock regeneration (**AC9**, **AC10**, and the `bin/`/`tests/` entries of **AC13**'s allow-list) — the auxiliary bookkeeping half, whose absence costs the reflection nothing operationally because the four answers are recorded on the board either way; on the trigger it goes to a fresh issue carrying **both rounds' findings as its requirement list**, never re-derived there, and the digest keeps exactly its current output shape. **Never dropped**: the reflection duty text and the disposition rule in the two skills, with their canonical block and registry row (**AC2**–**AC6**). That is #346's own substance; there is no carve-out to execute for it, so its defeat across two consecutive rounds is evidence about the design premise rather than about implementation craft, and the task **stops and returns to planning**.

## Input space

**Reachable input classes** — the situations this norm, this rule and this flag really meet, which the shipped text and the flag must handle correctly:

- A firing where the task's never-dropped set is entirely green, every finding in the class targets one auxiliary component, and exactly one pre-priced disposition exists for it. This is T-1093's live firing and the worked example's own case.
- A firing where **two or more** auxiliary components are in play and the pre-commitment recorded an ordered drop list over them — the plural case two specs in this corpus already carry (`.shell-team/specs/T-1093-verification-ceiling.md:50`, `docs/loop-engineering/tier2-subject-harness.md:106–112`). The rule executes the disposition for the earliest such component in that order, one firing at a time.
- A firing where the finding class targets an item in the never-dropped set. Escalation, always; the task stops and returns to planning rather than being patched.
- A firing where the component is auxiliary but the pre-commitment priced no disposition for it. Escalation, always.
- A firing where the pre-priced disposition is itself destructive or irreversible (a history rewrite, a deletion of work not recoverable from git). Escalation, always — this is one of the three standing human gates and the rule never reaches past it.
- A firing where an item of the never-dropped set is **not** green. Escalation, always: the reflection's first question is precisely what distinguishes a spiral around bookkeeping from a task whose primary value is still broken.
- A firing whose finding set is **empty** — a `STOP:budget_exhausted` or `STOP:guard_error` reached before any rework round was ever classified, which the loop can genuinely produce because those two `STOP:` reasons do not require a classified finding to fire. Conditions (2) and (3) are vacuously true over an empty set, so the rule fails closed: escalation, always, never execution.
- A firing whose findings exist but are **not yet classified** into root-cause classes. Same disposition and for the same reason: an unclassified finding cannot be shown to target an auxiliary component, so the predicate cannot be evaluated and escalation is the answer rather than a pass.
- A **mixed** firing — some findings classified against auxiliary components, at least one left unclassified. Escalation, and by condition (2) rather than by the exception sentence: condition (2) is a universal claim over **every** finding in the firing class, which an unclassified member cannot satisfy, so a mixed set fails the predicate outright. Named here because a literal reading of "empty or unclassified" would not obviously reach a set that is only partly unclassified, and the answer should not depend on that reading.
- A firing where more than one escalation condition holds at once (the never-dropped set is hit **and** an item of it is not green). Escalation either way; the fixed precedence order in the frozen Goal settles only which single audit value is recorded.
- A spec carrying **no** pre-commitment at all — every task predating this norm, and any adopter task that writes none. Question (3) answers no, so the escalation path is taken and today's behaviour is preserved exactly.
- For the digest flag: an invocation omitting `--reflection` (every existing caller, and every adopter's existing script), an invocation carrying exactly one of the five enum values, and an invocation carrying an out-of-enum value, a missing value or a duplicate flag — the last three fail closed with exit 2 and empty stdout, the shape `bin/rework-digest.sh` already uses for every other flag.

**Out-of-scope synthetic extremes** — named concretely, so an adversarial gate has a declared boundary to push against and the engineer has grounding to decline:

- A free-text or unbounded reflection payload on the digest, and any argument shaped to smuggle a verdict label or an `AC<digits>` token through the field. The field is a closed five-value enum; the script's own signature self-check is the backstop, and no further sanitizer is designed for inputs the enum cannot express.
- Ever-larger pre-commitments — a drop list of ten or fifty ordered components, nested droppable sub-components, or a disposition whose execution itself fires a second trigger. The plural case is protected at the cardinality the corpus actually contains (two); a synthetic escalation of that count is not protected and no criterion here grows with it.
- A pre-commitment whose recorded drop order is ambiguous, contradictory or absent while claiming to exist. That is a defect in the spec that wrote it, caught by the freeze sweep for that task, and this norm does not attempt to arbitrate it at firing time.
- An adversarial or forged board record — a fabricated set of four answers, a loop-escape record naming a disposition no pre-commitment priced. The party writing that record is the trusted principal; nothing here authenticates it, and this is stated as a limit rather than defended against.
- Concurrency: two firing points reached in the same turn, or a firing inside a declared `concurrent-review-window`. The rule is written for one firing at a time; the T-1080 discard carve-out is untouched and no interaction between the two is designed here.
- Any claim about the norm's *effect* — how many interruptions it saves, how often it fires correctly, whether an orchestrator obeys it. Unmeasurable from this repository at this task's scale and explicitly out of scope, in either direction.
