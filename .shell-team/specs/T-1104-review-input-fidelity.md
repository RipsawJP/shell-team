## Problem

The loop gates on the cross-provider review's **output** — a verdict token, a findings list, a synthesis ledger — while the review's **input** is unrecorded. Nothing in a review record says what argv was actually handed to the executor, whether that round's verdict-scope and severity-calibration briefing was carried into the invocation or only into the agent that wrote it, or whether a given pass generated its findings independently or was handed a prior round's findings to confirm. The consequence is that a verdict cannot be re-read later for what it was a verdict *on*: two rounds' verdicts are compared as if the judge and its brief held still, and neither is recorded. Worse, the shipped naming rule actively destroys the evidence — it forbids a per-round filename, so round 2's raw captures overwrite round 1's, and the record's own trail of what each round read is gone by the time anyone would want it.

Issue **#335** names three gaps measured during T-1086, and each is a distinct mechanism rather than three views of one: (1) the per-round briefing at `skills/run/SKILL.md:98` reaches the reviewer *agent* but cannot reach the primary executor pass, which has no prompt channel; (2) a confirmation pass and a seeded one are indistinguishable in the record, because no field states a pass's role; (3) raw captures are overwritten across rounds. This task ships the record leg: a per-pass grammar, a fail-closed checker over it, and a formalized per-round capture stem. It does not ship the executor-capability leg — whether `codex exec … review --base` can be given a scope preamble at all is a Non-goal below and goes to a fresh issue.

it exits 2 rather than 0 or 1 on an input it cannot evaluate (an unreadable record, a non-regular occupant at the record path, an unresolvable reviews directory, a usage error); it **never judges the content** of the verbatim invocation beyond non-emptiness and single-line-ness, so two records differing only in that field's free text both pass; `bin/close-out.sh` invokes it unconditionally as a sibling subprocess, mapping `1` to a board-content refusal that leaves the board byte-untouched and every other non-zero to `die`; the shipped naming rule at `agents/codex-reviewer.md:212` is **amended, not supplemented**, so a per-round raw capture is the sanctioned form rather than a forbidden one; and an adopter reading the shipped documentation can tell what each field means, what the two closed vocabularies admit, what the verbatim field may and may not contain, and what this mechanism does not guarantee.

**The naming rule is amended, and the amendment has two halves that move in opposite directions.** `agents/codex-reviewer.md:212` today forbids a per-round filename outright, and that prohibition is the direct cause of gap 3: obeying it means round 2's `--publish` overwrites round 1's canonical pair, so the record's own trail of what each round read is destroyed by the rule meant to keep it findable. The rule's stated ground — "the orchestrator's completion-detection greps these canonical basenames in that resolved dir" — is **implemented by no script**: measured first-hand, the literal `codex-primary` occurs outside `<reviews>/` in exactly five files, `.shell-team/todo.md`, `agents/codex-reviewer.md`, `tests/codex-skeleton-hygiene/run.sh` and two closed specs (`T-1030-reviewer-board-write-boundary.md`, `T-1036-wording-batch-141-143-144.md`), and in no file under `bin/`. So the cost of amending it is prose plus proving the hygiene suite's eight placeholder-stem literals unmoved, not a rewrite of a detection mechanism. **First half — formalize the round suffix.** The canonical stem becomes `T-NNN-<pass-name>` on a task's **first** round of a given pass family and `T-NNN-<pass-name>-r<N>` from the second round onward, never a bare stem once a second round exists — deliberately the identical convention `:195` already fixes for the verdict token's `(round <N>)` tail, so the record's verdict tail and its raw stem follow one rule rather than two. **Second half — normalize the pass name.** The pass-name vocabulary stays **closed at the three stems this file already names** (`codex-primary`, `codex-adversarial`, `codex-specreview`); T-1103's live `codex-impl-rN` is a deviation from that set and is normalized *out* rather than admitted, because a pass name invented per task defeats the purpose of a canonical basename in the first place, and because the three shipped names already partition the three modes the file defines. The two halves are recorded as one decision with opposite directions on purpose: the corpus's evidence supports the suffix and contradicts the free pass name, and treating both the same way would either re-forbid what practice has settled or bless a per-task vocabulary nobody can read.

**What the pass-role label distinguishes, and why it is a property of the invocation.** `generation` and `confirmation` describe the **invocation**, not the reviewer's activity: a pass whose argv names no prior finding is `generation` — the executor forms its findings from the artifact alone — and a pass whose argv names specific prior findings for the executor to check is `confirmation`. Fixing the label to the argv rather than to the outcome is what makes it legible beside the verbatim field a reader can compare it against, and it is what makes issue #335's gap 2 answerable at all: "a confirmation pass is indistinguishable from a seeded one" is a question about what was handed in, and only the invocation answers it. The checker validates membership in the closed set and nothing further; whether a label is *truthful* against its own verbatim field is a reader's judgment and a reviewer's finding, never a checker's verdict, because deciding it requires reading a free-form prompt for meaning.

**The three fields' verbatim half is this task's highest-value PII surface, and the grammar bounds it rather than trusting it.** The `executor-invocation` field's value is the **executor argv as invoked and nothing else**, rendered on one physical line with embedded newlines written as the two-character sequence `\n` — the same joining discipline `:109`'s `- Codex CLI:` field already fixes for multi-line output. Four things it must **not** carry, stated as a contract rather than a hope: an environment dump or any variable's expanded value beyond the argv itself; a credential, token, key or authentication header; an absolute path outside the repository — a home-directory path or a `$TMPDIR` session root in particular, since `bin/check-pii-shapes.sh`'s `home-path`, `home-encoded` and `temp-session` shapes fire on exactly those and a record carrying one is a finding on the pull request that lands it; and any operator or account identity. Two mechanisms back the contract and neither is new code inside this checker: `bin/check-pii-shapes.sh` runs **diff-scoped over the pull request** at `.github/workflows/check-handoff.yml:241`–`:242`, so a committed record is screened for those shapes on the change that lands it, and no second PII implementation ships here — a duplicate would drift from the one this repository maintains. And `bin/check-review-input.sh` **never echoes the field's bytes** in any refusal message, naming only the record, the pass id and the refusal token, which is `bin/check-commit-identity.sh:30`–`:33`'s DP-3 discipline applied to a field with the same property: a report that echoed it would turn a control into a leak in a public CI log.

**What this mechanism does not close — `disclosed, not closed`, eleven items.** (i) It records what was **invoked**, never what a model **received**: no judgment over committed bytes distinguishes an invocation whose briefing reached the executor's context from one where it did not, and the primary pass has no prompt channel at all, so its honest briefing-fidelity value is `not-carried` and the gap stays open rather than papered over. (ii) It does not judge whether a briefing was **adequate** — scope and calibration are prose, and their quality is a reader's judgment. (iii) It does not verify a `pass-role` label against its own verbatim field; a mislabelled pass passes the checker and is a reviewer's finding. (iv) It does not verify that the recorded argv is the argv that **ran**: nothing in a git repository binds a recorded string to a process, so a transcription error or a fabrication is invisible here. (v) It cannot see whether the raw file a `raw-capture` field names **exists**, because `/.gitignore:15`–`:17` makes it untracked; a stem naming nothing is conformant to this checker. (vi) It cannot distinguish a record that legitimately has no passes from one whose passes nobody recorded — the zero-field record exits 0 by design, which is the same limit `bin/check-spec-review.sh` discloses for its own election read, and the price of being forward-only over a corpus of already-committed records. (vii) It does not retrofit or re-derive fields for any record already committed, and no criterion below asks anyone to; fabricating values nobody measured would be worse than the gap. (viii) It does not detect a **collision across records** — two tasks naming the same stem — because a stem is required to carry its own record's task id and that prefix rule is the whole of the cross-record protection. (ix) It does not order passes, number rounds, or verify that a round series has no gap; the per-pass id is opaque on purpose, since one record spans two mode families whose round numbers restart independently. (x) It does not lock, re-read or otherwise guard against a concurrent write to the record while it reads, so a record edited mid-run is out of scope. (xi) It does not make the executor-capability question answerable: whether the primary pass can be given a scope preamble at all is out of scope here and goes to a fresh issue, and until that issue lands, gap 1's closure is that the gap is **recorded** rather than that it is fixed.

## Input space

**Reachable input classes** — what the real corpus and real usage produce, enumerated positively so the boundary is falsifiable:

1. **A review record carrying zero input-fidelity fields.** Every review record committed in this repository today (the population `git ls-files -- <reviews>/T-*.md` enumerates) is in this class, and so is every record in every adopter repository at the moment they upgrade. Must exit 0.
2. **A record with one verdict section and one complete pass block** — the ordinary single-round, single-pass case.
3. **A record spanning many verdict sections across two mode families whose round numbers restart independently.** Measured maximum in this corpus: `.shell-team/reviews/T-1103.md`, nine sections (five `## Spec review` rounds plus four `## Codex review — round N (implementation)` sections).
4. **A section carrying one pass or two.** Measured: implementation sections carry a primary and, when a blocker or major fired, an adversarial companion; T-1103's fourth implementation section carries a primary alone.
5. **Both verdict heading families** — `### Codex Review verdict:` and `### Codex Spec-Review verdict:` — each with the four verdict tails `:195` fixes (bare and `(round <N>)`, both verdict tokens).
6. **Pass ids and raw-capture stems drawn from `[a-z0-9-]+`**, including the three shipped pass names, the `-r<N>` round suffix, and the historical spellings the corpus already carries (`-round2`, `-round5`, `-round2-adversarial`, `-r2`…`-r5`, `-impl-rN`).
7. **An `executor-invocation` value that is a real `codex exec` argv**: read-only sandbox flags, a `--cd` path, `--json -o <path>`, a `--base <ref>`, and — for the adversarial and spec-review passes — a long free-form prompt containing quotes, shell metacharacters, backslash escapes, JSON braces and instruction-like prose.
8. **Records reached under both supported layouts and under a `$TEAM_RUN_BASE` override**, resolved through `bin/team-paths.sh`.
9. **A record whose own task id differs from the `--task` argument**, and a record carrying a stem prefixed with a different task's id.
10. **A record path that is unreadable, a directory, or a dangling symlink**, and a reviews directory that does not resolve.
11. **Near-miss vocabulary spellings** a human writing the field by hand actually produces: a capitalized first letter, a trailing colon, a truncation (`confirm`, `notcarried`), an extension (`confirmation-pass`, `not-carried-yet`), and an empty value.

**Out-of-scope synthetic extremes** — named concretely, and declined:

1. **Records with more than a few dozen verdict sections or pass blocks.** The measured corpus maximum is nine sections; a record with hundreds or thousands is not protected, and no criterion above asserts behaviour at that scale.
2. **Non-ASCII pass ids or raw-capture stems.** Refused by the charset rule rather than folded, normalized or transliterated; no confusable analysis is attempted, so two stems that render identically through bidirectional-override or zero-width characters are out of scope beyond what the charset refuses.
3. **An `executor-invocation` value that is a single physical line of extreme length** (megabytes). The grammar requires one physical line; how long that line may be is not bounded here.
4. **Concurrent writes to the record while the checker reads it.** No locking, no re-read, no TOCTOU hardening — a record edited mid-run is out of scope, and item (x) of the Goal's enumeration discloses it.
5. **A record whose bytes are not valid text in the operator's locale**, or which carries a byte-order mark or CRLF line endings throughout. The checker runs under `LC_ALL=C` and reads lines; a mixed-encoding record is out of scope.
6. **Process-level attestation that the recorded argv is the argv that ran.** No channel in a git repository binds a recorded string to an executed process, and item (iv) discloses it rather than protecting against it.
7. **Adversarial content inside the verbatim field intended to influence a later reader or a later model** (prompt-injection-shaped prose). The checker deliberately does not judge that content — **AC8** measures exactly that — and the mitigation is the field contract plus the diff-scoped PII gate, not a content filter.

## Pre-commitment

- never-dropped: the **checker's presence-and-grammar core** — the four fields, their per-pass completeness and non-duplication, and the pass-id charset (**AC5**). Its defeat means the record grammar is decorative and the whole task delivers nothing readable.

- never-dropped: the **forward-only guarantee** (**AC3**) — every already-committed review record exits 0, unmodified. Its defeat breaks every adopter's existing corpus on upgrade, and no benefit of this task is worth that.
- never-dropped: the **no-echo discipline** (**AC9**). Its defeat converts this task's own highest-value PII surface into a leak in a public CI log, which is worse than not shipping the record at all.
