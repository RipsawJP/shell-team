## Problem

Five cross-provider rounds in the v1.5.0 and v1.6.0 trains each deferred a defect that lives inside a *merged, frozen* artifact: a locator naming the wrong record, a settled-decision paragraph left stale by a sibling's re-freeze, a frozen prediction contradicted by its own measurement, eight `check:` anchors whose match does not close at the subject it locates, and one categorical claim that is over-broad. Each was correctly held non-blocking, and each was correctly routed to pm-spec rather than patched by an engineer, because the bytes are hashed. The result is a backlog that no single task owns: every one of them is cheap, none of them is urgent, and the repair route is the same for all of them.

Two further items joined the pile from a different direction. T-1038's review surfaced two acceptance criteria that were **already red at their own base ref** — not because anything went wrong in the task that wrote them, but because each states a claim about what changed since a named commit, and later tasks legitimately changed more. Left alone they stay red forever, and a permanently red criterion trains every later reader to skip it.

Nothing in the loop drains a backlog of this shape on its own. A batched pass can, and the risk it carries is the one every batch carries: it can look complete while quietly leaving items behind. So the coverage has to be stated, and the items deliberately **not** covered have to be named with their reasons, or "the backlog is empty" is a false claim.
