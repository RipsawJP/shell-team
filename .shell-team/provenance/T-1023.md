- decision: the surrounding prose in `docs/loop-engineering/playbook-block-size-deferral.md` (especially the `## What this record is not` section, where AC2's and AC7's forbidden-vocabulary traps are named to bite hardest) states each disclaimer in the affirmative shape the frozen claim lines already use — "the mechanism is real, and its harm has never been measured, in either direction" — rather than in a negated shape such as "this is not a claim that the harm is small" or "this record carries no deadline".
  reason: multiple wordings would convey the same intended meaning to a human reader, but AC2's negative grep runs case-insensitively over the whole record for `harmless|harm is (small|low|minor|limited)|no real harm` **including inside a negation**, and AC7's negative grep runs case-insensitively for `deadline|due date|next sprint|scheduled for|on a schedule|assignee|...`. A negated sentence built from one of those tokens (e.g. "not harmless", "no deadline governs it") reads fine to a human and is exactly the string those greps are built to catch, per the spec's own Notes for engineer. Restating each disclaimer the way `claim-1` and `claim-5` already phrase it (affirmatively, without the forbidden token) was the only wording space available that preserves both AC2/AC7's pass condition and the record's actual meaning; this affects the frozen intent block's own acceptance criteria (AC2, AC7), so it is recorded rather than treated as ordinary prose styling.
  grounding: `.shell-team/specs/T-1023-block-size-deferral-record.md`, AC2 and AC7 `check:` lines (the forbidden-token sets), and `## Notes for engineer` → "Vocabulary traps in the record's prose" (the explicit "write it the way claim-1 already does" / "say signal-driven, not scheduled" instruction); the produced doc's own `## What this record is not` section, where the affirmative phrasing is applied.
