- decision: an unreadable `templates/binding-adapters.txt` (T-1054's shipped adapter allowlist, read-only by `bin/check-adapter.sh` to validate a definition's `adapter`/`provider` pair) is refused `contract-unreadable` rather than a new, 22nd refusal token.
  reason: the spec's own Goal states the validator has "a closed 21-token refusal matrix" and `## Refusal matrix` enumerates exactly 21 rows; inventing a 22nd token for this one absent-input case would contradict that frozen count. Two members of the existing 21 are plausible homes — `contract-unreadable` ("the contract registry is absent, not a regular file, or unreadable") and `definitions-unreadable` ("the definitions directory is absent or unreadable"). The allowlist is neither the envelope contract registry nor the definitions directory by name, but it is, like the contract registry, a plugin-shipped, read-only reference this checker cannot evaluate anything meaningful without — an absent allowlist means no `adapter`/`provider` cross-check in this script can be judged at all, the same class of "cannot evaluate the input" as an absent contract registry, so it was folded into `contract-unreadable` rather than `definitions-unreadable` (which specifically names the per-adapter definitions directory, a different artifact).
  grounding: `.shell-team/specs/T-1055-adapter-envelope.md` `## Refusal matrix` (the closed 21-row table and its `contract-unreadable`/`definitions-unreadable` condition text) and Goal ("closed 21-token refusal matrix"); `bin/check-adapter.sh`'s `load_adapters_registry` function (the `refuse contract-unreadable 2 ...` call on an unreadable `$ADAPTERS_REGISTRY`).
- decision: the channel vocabulary the shipped `templates/task-envelope.txt` declares is nine tokens (`argv`, `prompt`, `stdin`, `stdout`, `stderr`, `file`, `board`, `exit-status`, `not-carried`) rather than the eight the spec's `## Notes for engineer` suggests (`argv`, `prompt`, `stdin`, `file`, `board`, `exit-status`, `stderr`, `not-carried` — no `stdout`).
  reason: the spec explicitly states the suggested vocabularies are "deliberately outside the frozen block so the measurement can change them" and that AC2 "constrains the vocabularies' shape ... and never their membership." Modeling the generalized invocation pattern (a headless CLI call reporting its result on process stdout, e.g. `claude -p --output-format json` or `codex exec -o <file>`) needed a channel distinct from `file` for the case where the executor's own final answer is read directly off its stdout stream rather than a separately captured file — the two shipped adapters actually differ on this axis (`claude-cli`'s verdict/usage/produced-artifacts are declared `stdout`; `codex-cli`'s equivalent fields are declared `file`, matching this repository's own `codex-capture.sh` convention of redirecting the whole JSONL stream to a file). Omitting a `stdout` channel would have forced `claude-cli`'s definition to spell either an inaccurate `file` claim or an uninformative `not-carried` for fields it genuinely does carry.
  grounding: `.shell-team/specs/T-1055-adapter-envelope.md` `## Notes for engineer` ("Suggested vocabularies, deliberately outside the frozen block ... Channels: `argv`, `prompt`, `stdin`, `file`, `board`, `exit-status`, `stderr`, `not-carried`") and DP5 ("How the envelope reaches the executor is declared as a `carries <field> <channel>` mapping"); `templates/task-envelope.txt`'s `channel` rows; `templates/adapters/claude-cli.txt` vs `templates/adapters/codex-cli.txt`'s differing `carries verdict/usage/produced-artifacts` channel values.
- decision: the shipped `effort-mechanism` vocabulary is exactly three tokens (`none`, `cli-flag`, `cli-config-override`) rather than the five the spec's `## Notes for engineer` suggests (also `harness-setting`, `api-parameter`).
  reason: AC2 requires only "at least two `effort-mechanism` rows exist, one of them exactly `none` and at least one of them not `none`" — membership is unconstrained. The two shipped adapters' measured mechanisms (DP7) are exactly `cli-flag` (claude-cli's dedicated `--effort` flag) and `cli-config-override` (codex-cli's generic `-c model_reasoning_effort=<value>` override) plus `none` (the closed-set anchor AC2 requires); `harness-setting` and `api-parameter` describe mechanisms neither shipped adapter actually uses. Declaring an unused vocabulary member would be a claim about a mechanism this task never measured against any real provider — the spec's own DP7 language ("measured against the real provider ... never guessed") argues against shipping a guessed, unused token in the very registry DP7 governs. A later adapter needing one of the other two tokens adds it then, at no cost (AC2 pins shape, not membership).
  grounding: `.shell-team/specs/T-1055-adapter-envelope.md` AC2 (the effort-mechanism row-count/`none`-presence clauses) and DP7 ("measured against the real provider ... never guessed") and `## Notes for engineer` ("None of these is frozen; AC2 constrains the vocabularies' shape ... and never their membership"); `templates/task-envelope.txt`'s `effort-mechanism` rows; `templates/adapters/claude-cli.txt` and `templates/adapters/codex-cli.txt`'s own `effort-mechanism` values.
- decision: in `bin/check-adapter.sh`'s definition parser, a second `carries` row for a field already covered is refused `duplicate-field` unconditionally — including when the second row names the identical channel as the first, not only when the two rows disagree.
  reason: the spec's `## Refusal matrix` states `duplicate-field`'s condition as "a single-valued definition directive appears more than once," and lists no separate token for a conflicting-vs-identical repeated `carries` row; the "Coverage the suite must carry beyond the criteria" note names "a `carries` row appearing twice for one field" as a case the suite must cover, without specifying which of the two sub-shapes (same channel repeated, or a genuinely conflicting channel) it means. Treating `carries` as conceptually one-row-per-field (matching AC4's own coverage check, which expects exactly one `carries` row per contract field per definition) and refusing ANY second occurrence — rather than silently tolerating an identical repeat and only refusing a conflicting one — was chosen because it is the simpler, single rule to implement and test, and because a hand-authored definition that repeats a `carries` line at all (even identically) is exactly the kind of accidental duplication DP12's threat model (accidents, not hostile input) exists to catch early rather than silently absorb.
  grounding: `.shell-team/specs/T-1055-adapter-envelope.md` `## Refusal matrix` (`duplicate-field`'s condition text) and `## Notes for engineer` ("a `carries` row appearing twice for one field") and AC4 ("Every `field` token in the canonical contract has a `carries <field> <channel>` row ... " — singular); `bin/check-adapter.sh`'s `validate_definition` function (`carries_field` array membership check preceding `duplicate-field` refusal, with no channel-equality exception); `tests/check-adapter/run.sh`'s `ca-duplicate-field-carries` case.
- decision: `bin/check-adapter.sh --binding` re-validates a bound adapter's definition file once per role that binds it (no per-adapter result cache across the loop), rather than caching each adapter's parsed capability facts after its first validation in the loop.
  reason: this repository's `tests/check-refreeze-class/run.sh` test-recipe entry states the classifier's "bash-3.2 compatibility bar (indexed arrays only — no associative arrays, no `mapfile`)" applies repo-wide to every `tests/*/run.sh` this shell invokes, and the same constraint governs `bin/` scripts sharing this convention. A per-adapter cache keyed by adapter token needs either an associative array (unavailable under bash 3.2) or dynamic variable names via `declare -g`/`eval` (bash 4.2+ only, and `eval` is separately disallowed by this script's own no-`eval`/no-`source` static self-check, `ca-no-eval-source-static`). Re-validating a small, plugin-shipped definition file — at most a handful of lines, at most six times (one per inner-loop role) — costs nothing observable and keeps every array in the script indexed, matching `bin/check-refreeze-class.sh`'s and `bin/check-intent.sh`'s own documented compatibility bar rather than introducing the first associative-array or `declare -g` usage in this repository's `bin/` scripts.
  grounding: `.shell-team/test-recipe.md`'s `T-1028` entry ("The classifier's own bash-3.2 compatibility bar (indexed arrays only — no associative arrays, no `mapfile`) applies to this suite too"); `bin/check-adapter.sh`'s no-eval/no-source static self-check (mirrored in `tests/check-adapter/run.sh`'s `ca-no-eval-source-static` case) and the `binding` mode's dispatch block (`validate_definition` called unconditionally inside the `while IFS= read -r bline` loop, no cache array).

- decision: (v2, round 2 — **SUPERSEDES the v1 decision immediately above**, which folded an unreadable `templates/binding-adapters.txt` into `contract-unreadable`) an unreadable or malformed adapter allowlist is now refused with `registry-unreadable` (2) / `registry-malformed` (2) — `bin/check-binding.sh`'s own tokens, at the same exit codes, for the same conditions (two-token rows, character class, length cap, no adapter token declared twice) — and `contract-unreadable` now names the envelope contract registry (`templates/task-envelope.txt`) alone.
  reason: round 1's Blocker (`.shell-team/reviews/T-1055.md`) reproduced, against the real shipped `templates/binding-adapters.txt`, that folding the allowlist's malformedness into `contract-unreadable` meant this checker never actually validated the allowlist's CONTENT (only its readability) — a three-token row appended to the real file left `bin/check-adapter.sh` reporting `valid` at exit `0` while `bin/check-binding.sh`, parsing the identical file, correctly refused `registry-malformed` at exit `2`. The v1 decision's own stated ground (an absent allowlist is "the same class of cannot-evaluate-the-input as an absent contract registry") is still correct for the UNREADABLE case, but the v1 implementation never extended that reasoning to the READABLE-but-malformed case at all — it only checked field count and `continue`d past anything else. The spec's own DP15 settles this: two checkers reading one file must not disagree about whether it is well-formed, so this checker now speaks `bin/check-binding.sh`'s own vocabulary for this one shared file rather than folding it into a different token that was never validated at the same strictness. This is not a repair of the OLD decision's reasoning (which was sound for what it addressed) — it is a strictly larger decision that supersedes it because the artifact's own scope (a plugin-shipped file this checker depends on but does not own the grammar of) turned out to need its own two-token vocabulary, matching the file's actual owner, rather than sharing a token with a different artifact (the envelope contract registry) whose unreadable-vs-malformed distinction is unrelated.
  grounding: `.shell-team/reviews/T-1055.md` (the Blocker finding, `load_adapters_registry`, and the ledger's `blocker` re-grading); `.shell-team/specs/T-1055-adapter-envelope.md` DP15 ("this supersedes the v1 implementation decision that folded an unreadable allowlist into `contract-unreadable`") and AC18 (the parity test); `bin/check-adapter.sh`'s rewritten `load_adapters_registry` function (now byte-identical in structure to `bin/check-binding.sh`'s own registry loader).
- decision: the shipped `status-value` set is exactly three tokens — `ok` (success), `refused` (failure), `error` (failure) — rather than a larger or provider-specific set.
  reason: DP14 requires exactly one `success` member and at least one `failure` member, with membership otherwise unconstrained ("this cut does not freeze the status vocabulary's membership" carried over from v1, now with a machine form). The spec's own `## Notes for engineer` (v1, still present) suggests "a success token plus a refusal token plus an error token" as the shape — three tokens, two kinds of failure distinguished by CAUSE (a content-level refusal vs. an infrastructure-level failure to complete at all), matching the same content-refusal-vs-cannot-evaluate split this checker's own exit-code convention already uses (1 vs 2). Reusing that already-established two-way split for the ENVELOPE's own status vocabulary, rather than inventing an unrelated taxonomy or a single generic `failure` token, costs nothing and keeps the two vocabularies (checker exit codes, envelope status values) conceptually aligned without literally coupling them (an envelope's `status` is a property of an INVOCATION result, not of this checker's own exit code).
  grounding: `.shell-team/specs/T-1055-adapter-envelope.md` DP14 ("exactly one row is marked `success`... at least one must be marked `failure`... the membership itself is not frozen") and `## Notes for engineer` ("Status values... one success token plus a refusal token plus an error token is the shape the contract document already describes"); `templates/task-envelope.txt`'s `status-value` rows; `docs/loop-engineering/task-envelope.md`'s `## The status vocabulary` table.
- decision: `templates/adapters/claude-cli.txt`'s `carries board-transition` row is changed from the retired `board` channel to `stdout`, matching that adapter's other `out` fields (`verdict`, `produced-artifacts`, `usage`), rather than to `file` or `not-carried`.
  reason: DP13 requires a real return-path channel now that `board` is retired. `claude-cli` is modeled as a headless, non-interactive CLI invocation (`claude -p --output-format json`) whose final answer — including whatever board-transition token a `writes` or `proposes` role reports — is read off process stdout, exactly like this adapter's other `out` fields; `file` would misdescribe the actual invocation shape (nothing here captures a separate file the way `codex-cli`'s wrapper does), and `not-carried` would be actively wrong for the `writes`/`proposes` roles this adapter legally binds (and would trip the new `authority-channel-conflict` join for every one of them). `stdout` is therefore not merely "a" legal channel but the one that actually describes this adapter's own invocation shape, consistent with the rest of its own `carries` table.
  grounding: `templates/adapters/claude-cli.txt` (the other `out`-field `carries` rows, all `stdout`); `docs/loop-engineering/task-envelope.md` `## Carries declares a return path, never an authority (DP13)` and the file's own inline comment above its `capability effort` block explaining the `stdout` choice; `.shell-team/specs/T-1055-adapter-envelope.md` DP13 (`authority-channel-conflict`'s condition).
- decision: (v3, round 3) the error-class completeness floor (V3) accepts a registry declaring MORE than the four required error-class tokens — it refuses only when one of the four (`executor-unavailable`, `capability-unsupported`, `invocation-failed`, `contract-violation`) is absent, never when an additional, unlisted token is also present.
  reason: the frozen `## Refusal matrix`'s own condition text for `contract-incomplete` states the failing condition as "any of the four required error-class tokens absent (non-emptiness is not the floor... round 2 reproduced an arbitrary single row passing)" — worded as a floor (each of the four must be present), not as a ceiling (no other token may exist). AC2's own mutation for this clause only exercises the floor side (removing one required token while leaving an unrelated row in its place); it does not exercise whether an extra, additional error-class token beyond the four is itself a violation. Two designs were viable: (a) a strict-equality check (the error-class set must be exactly the four, no more), or (b) a floor check (the four must all be present; extras are fine). (a) would foreclose a later task extending the error-class vocabulary without a re-freeze of this criterion, contradicting the spec's own stated design elsewhere (DP14's `status-value`/`effort-mechanism` vocabularies are explicitly "closed but not frozen... membership... unconstrained" beyond their required anchors) and the Refusal matrix's own error-class row, which says "more may be added later without cost." (b) was chosen as the reading consistent with both the refusal matrix's own condition wording and the vocabulary-extensibility pattern this spec already establishes for its other closed-but-extensible vocabularies.
  grounding: `.shell-team/specs/T-1055-adapter-envelope.md` `## Refusal matrix` (`contract-incomplete`'s condition text, "any of the four required error-class tokens absent... non-emptiness is not the floor") and `templates/task-envelope.txt`'s own header comment ("Four are required by AC2... more may be added later without cost"); `bin/check-adapter.sh`'s `load_contract` function (the `REQUIRED_ERRORCLASSES` presence loop, which checks membership of each required token in `ERRORCLASS_SET` rather than set equality).
