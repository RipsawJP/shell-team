- decision: Implement the round-guard scan as a single awk pass over the record (CR stripped inside awk, stem net by `index($0, stem) == 1`), with the bash side only reading the cap and mapping the awk result to the output and exit code, rather than a bash `while read` loop or a `sed` copy plus `grep`.
  reason: The close-out mode's header argues that a `while read` loop drops a final line with no newline; one awk pass reads every line including the unterminated one, needs no temp file, and keeps the closed grammar and the position check in one place. The grammar regex stays defined once in bash and is handed to awk through the environment so both modes share it.
  grounding: `.shell-team/specs/T-1160-spec-review-round-cap.md` `## Notes for engineer` (reuse the CR strip, the stem net and the no-`while read` rule; share the grammar) and **AC3**.

- decision: Compare a `(round N)` suffix to its position as a string after stripping leading zeros, instead of `10#` arithmetic.
  reason: `10#` on an over-wide digit run wraps or errors in bash, so a suffix wider than the integer range could be turned into a match; a string compare has no overflow and treats `02` and `08` as decimal. Behaviour on every fixture in the criterion is identical.
  grounding: `.shell-team/specs/T-1160-spec-review-round-cap.md` **AC12** (D2).
