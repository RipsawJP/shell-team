#### Major
- docs/loop-engineering/playbook-bullet-measurement.md:46-47 — `no-longer-needed candidate` and `covered-by-others` are the same arm pattern (control-none shows the failure shape; full and minus-one show none), so the arms cannot tell them apart. The stated reading of the first, that the model avoids the mistake without this entry, does not follow from its arms: control-none has no bullets and showed the failure, so the model does not avoid it alone and the other bullets are what cover it. The case where the model avoids the mistake alone (control-none shows nothing) is filed as `not-elicited` and stays `unknown`, which is the right conservative call but means the design as written can only identify covered-by-others, which is also the only reading that could name a `Superseded-by` successor. Fix: merge the two verdicts into one defined by that pattern with the covered-by-other-bullets reading, say plainly that a mistake the model avoids alone is indistinguishable from not-elicited, and identify the covering entry with a further arm if a successor is wanted.
- docs/loop-engineering/playbook-bullet-measurement.md:65-70 — the run, time and token estimates omit the `full` arm that the verdict table needs (a comparison against `full` is in every verdict). Re-derived with one shared three-repetition `full` arm: pilot 10x3 + 3 + 3 = 36 runs, not 33; full sweep 87x3 + 3 + 3 = 267, not 261. At the current sampled engineer means (13.20 minutes, 162,482 tokens, 34 rows) the pilot is 36 runs, about 7.9 hours and about 5.85 million tokens, against the stated 33, about seven and a half hours and about five and a third million; the sweep is about 59 hours and about 43.4 million tokens. Separately the note reuses the subject-01 harness but prices from the telemetry mean without saying why: that subject own recorded baseline is 304 s and 590 s per repetition with 85,718 and 116,854 tokens (`docs/loop-engineering/tier2-subject-harness.md:69-76`), roughly half the priced time and about 60 percent of the priced tokens. Either figure may be the better price; the note must state which it uses and why.
- docs/loop-engineering/playbook-bullet-measurement.md:49,97 with :45-47 — three binary repetitions cannot carry a removal proposal, and the note handling of small effects is the wrong way round. `## What this cannot measure` says a small real effect reads as `inconclusive`, but `inconclusive` is defined by arm disagreement or spread as large as the difference, and a detector that is binary per run has no defined spread. A still-needed lesson that fails in 30 percent of runs without it gives zero failures in three minus-one runs with probability 0.7^3 = 0.343, so with one control failure it reads `no-longer-needed candidate`, not `inconclusive`. Small effects therefore turn into removal proposals, not into unknowns. The phrase more runs than the full arm also has no stated threshold (1 of 3 against 0 of 3 qualifies as written). Fix: pre-declare a minimum difference and counts per verdict, and state that 0/3 against 0/3 is not evidence of no effect.
- docs/loop-engineering/playbook-bullet-measurement.md:13 versus :51 — the unit section says an `Applies-to: all` entry is a removal candidate only if no role shows an effect, but the promotion rule counts only the strata measured in. Tier B covers the engineer role only, so as written an entry can be proposed for whole-entry removal, taking its QA, PM and tech-lead renderings with it, with those strata unmeasured. Fix: require a conclusive result for every role the entry renders into, or limit a proposal to narrowing `Applies-to` to the unmeasured roles.
- docs/loop-engineering/playbook-bullet-measurement.md:99 and :113 — the note says whole-block effects cannot be observed and that the design as written does not produce a signal-1 observation, while Tier B defines a `control-none` arm (no bullets) and a `full` arm on the same subject scored by the same oracle. That pair is a whole-block contrast: if control-none outperforms full on the oracle, an injected block degrades the role, which is signal-1 of `docs/loop-engineering/playbook-block-size-deferral.md`. Fix: either state that the control-none versus full comparison is recorded and reported as a signal-1 observation when it appears (it still says nothing about block size as a line count), or state why the comparison is excluded. Claims 1-5 of the deferral record are otherwise not contradicted.
