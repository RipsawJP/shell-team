#### QA's specific question: is `malformed-record` an honest class token for a doubled sentinel?

Judged against D3/D6's frozen text directly, and independently confirmed by Codex's own live grep sweep of every other `malformed-record` use in the repo: **honest, not a misleading stretch.** Reasoning:

1. **The two textually-closer alternatives are actively worse, not just less apt.** `sentinel-with-verdicts`'s own name and D3's own text fix its meaning to "a sentinel AND at least one verdict co-occur" — false here (`v==0`); reusing it would tell a debugging adopter the wrong story about what happened. `duplicate-claim` is wired, by D5 and by the script's own `claims.$i`/`allclaims` code path, to a duplicated `- unit:` **claim** line specifically — a second `- sentinel:` line is not a second claim, and routing it through that check would misattribute the mechanism to the wrong phase entirely.

3. **`malformed-record`'s own binding in this spec is to a "shape" violation, and D3's "exactly one" is textually a shape/cardinality statement, not a co-occurrence one.** The Body-to-AC correspondence table ties `malformed-record` to D2's "three part-file line shapes" at the single-line grain (AC6's own fixture: a line matching none of the three shapes). D3 states what a *well-formed record set for one unit* looks like: `{v≥1, s=0}` or `{v=0, s=1}` — exactly two legal shapes. `{v=0, s=2}` matches neither. Reading "record" at the per-unit grain rather than strictly the per-line grain is a real widening of the token's grain, but it is not a contradiction of anything AC6 or any other frozen check line asserts (no frozen check exercises `s>1` at all, confirmed by grep), and it is squarely the same class of interpretive choice within the closed vocabulary that this task's own `--population`-unreadable-file precedent already resolved (see the provenance entry above).
