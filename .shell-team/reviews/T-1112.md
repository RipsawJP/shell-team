### My own synthesis of Codex's findings

I independently re-verified every finding above directly against the spec's own embedded `- check:` bodies (not against Codex's paraphrase) before scoring severity. Two calibration notes govern the severities below: (a) this task's own launch brief asks for **full adversarial depth on the rule text and the README claim change**, and only **value-correctness depth on inline check lines** in general — so a finding that is really about whether the shipped *rule text* (in `phase-multiplexing.md` or the READMEs) will read as intended is held to a higher bar than a finding that is purely about a check body's own substring looseness; (b) per this repository's own methodology, almost every AC in this entire spec corpus verifies via literal/substring grep rather than a semantic parser — a finding that says "a substring check cannot prove an arbitrary rewording is absent" is an inherent, corpus-wide limitation, not a defect unique to this spec, and I do not escalate that generic critique to Major on its own.

Applying that split:

- **Finding 1 (AC1, vacuous-pass) → Major.** Verified directly: the check extracts `$T/base` and `$P` for the byte-identity clauses, but the two trigger-literal existence checks (`grep -qF -- "$s" "$P"`) search the *whole file*, never the extracted trigger line (`$T/rh`-style extraction is not used here, unlike AC3).
