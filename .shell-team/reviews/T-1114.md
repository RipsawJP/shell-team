### My own synthesis of Codex's findings

**Finding 1 (rename-source invisibility) — upheld as Major, and this is the load-bearing finding of this round.** My own independent testing above reproduces every claim in Codex's finding 1 exactly, including the specific point that the identical gap exists in the *committed-range* read (`base...HEAD`), which Codex's own tool trace also verified live rather than reasoning about it abstractly. Codex's generalization beyond my own initial framing — that a **disallowed-source-to-allowed-destination** rename is the sharper attack shape (a task quietly moves/deletes a file it was never permitted to touch, and the union reports a clean scope because the source vanishes) — is correct and is the more dangerous of the two directions, since it hides an unauthorized deletion rather than merely under-reporting a benign one. The spec is not unaware of rename-source visibility in general (**A-7** and **AC16(a)** both name the question, and **AC5**'s own prose explicitly notes "the disallowed destination is present either way" as the reason its own specific test is unaffected), but neither **A-7** nor **AC16(a)** as worded requires testing the **reverse** direction specifically — AC16(a)'s six named mutation cases include only "a staged rename" (singular, undifferentiated by direction), and AC5's own live proof only exercises the allowed→disallowed direction. A diligent engineer following AC16(a) literally could reasonably conclude, from AC5's own successful test, that no `--no-renames` change is needed — and never discover the reverse-direction gap, because nothing in the spec's frozen text asks for it. This is squarely a mandate item 1 concern ("is the fixed union complete") and the Goal's own closing claim — "so the union is complete rather than merely wider" — is not fully supported as written; that exact phrase, plus "unbroken chain," ships verbatim into `agents/pm-spec.md` (required literals on **AC1**'s shipped bullet), so this is not merely a spec-review nitpick but prose that will become permanent, adopter-facing authoring guidance overclaiming completeness it does not fully have.
