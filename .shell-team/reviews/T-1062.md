#### Major
- `.shell-team/specs/T-1062-release-notes-compare-link.md:48` (AC1 check line) — The bullet-boundary extraction (`awk '/^<!-- BEGIN release-body -->$/{exit} /^- /{f=(index($0,"## Install / update")>0)} f'`) only resets `f` on a line that literally begins `^- `. A paragraph placed directly after the target bullet — separated by a blank line, not itself a new `- `/`*`/`+` list item — leaves `f` true and is silently absorbed into the extracted "bullet," so the literal-presence checks still pass even though the exception has moved out of the actual Markdown list item and back into the "footnote" placement AC1 exists to reject. This is not hypothetical: the engineer's own AC7(a) mutation-1 note records that exactly this shape ("a naive 'paragraph right after the bullet' attempt") **failed to redden AC1**, and they had to move the paragraph between two *other* bullets instead to get a red — i.e., the gap was found empirically during this task's own mutation testing and worked around rather than fixed. Confirmed by direct read of the check line, corroborated independently by both the primary and adversarial Codex passes.
  - Suggested fix (mechanics only, semantics of AC1 unchanged): terminate the extraction at the actual Markdown list-item boundary — reset `f` on a blank line followed by non-indented content, or on any CommonMark unordered-list marker (`^[[:space:]]{0,3}[-+*][[:space:]]`), not only on the literal `^- ` this criterion happens to use elsewhere.
- `.shell-team/specs/T-1062-release-notes-compare-link.md:54` (AC3 check line) — The "outside-the-bullet survival" clause is one-directional and whole-file-scoped: it extracts the branch-point preamble lines outside the target bullet, then asserts each still appears *somewhere* in the working file (`grep -qxF -- "$l" "$F"`, searched against the whole file, not the corresponding region). Two consequences, both real: (a) a brand-new paragraph or bullet inserted anywhere else in the preamble, while every original line survives untouched, still passes — so AC3 does not actually establish its own stated property ("one bullet moved and nothing else did"); (b) an untouched rule can be *relocated* — deleted from its original spot and pasted verbatim inside the target bullet or after the body — and still "survive" by the whole-file membership test, even though the criterion's own prose is about confinement, not mere presence. Confirmed by direct read of the check line, corroborated independently by both Codex passes.
  - Suggested fix: extract the non-target preamble from *both* the base and the working file with the same boundary rule, and compare those two bounded extractions for equality (not membership) — this catches additions and relocations, not just deletions.
- Both AC1 and AC3 sit inside this task's own frozen intent block (`68c4b19cf9771b1439825bbea1c9a844db8ca2a9`, no re-freeze in any round). Tightening their check-line mechanics without touching the criterion's stated meaning ("the exception lives in the same bullet," "only the one preamble bullet moves") is a class-M repair, not a class-B one — it does not touch the Goal sentence, Non-goals, a criterion's prose, or Input space, and it is this task's own criterion rather than another task's merged intent, so `## Non-goals`'s "no repair … of any merged criterion" clause does not apply to it. This is squarely a rework-round fix, not a fast-follow.
