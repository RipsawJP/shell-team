#### Major

- **`docs/loop-engineering/phase-multiplexing.md`'s Worked substitution and `implement — tier2` dispatch-rule trigger compare a rework-rounds threshold against the wrong series.** The break-even parameter being solved for is explicitly "the expected rework rounds avoided" (`tier2-rework-rounds-avoided`), yet the Worked substitution paragraph and the `dispatch-rule: implement — tier2` trigger both compare the computed thresholds (≈0.739 at N=2, ≈0.928 at N=3) against the raw `rounds-to-approve-series` (range 1–9), not against the rework-only series (`rounds-to-approve − 1`, range 0–8 — round 1 is an unavoidable pass under either tier, so it is never itself "avoided"). I recomputed the rework-only series myself from the same eleven recorded values (3,2,1,9,3,4,2,1,4,5,3 → rework 2,1,0,8,2,3,1,0,3,4,2): two of eleven recorded tasks (T-1058, T-1064) have zero rework, so neither threshold is attainable for that task class, and only 9/11 (≈81.8%) of the corpus actually clears both thresholds — not "all eleven," as the sentence "both thresholds sit below the smallest number of rounds any of these eleven tasks has ever needed to reach approval (1)" implies by using the wrong denominator. The overall favorable direction survives (9/11 is still a real majority), but the specific universal-coverage claim in the note's own prose is incorrect, and it is exactly the kind of "conclusions' direction" claim the `### Verdict scope` marks as gating. Codex's primary pass caught this independently (P1 #2) and its adversarial pass confirmed my own 9/11 count exactly. Fix: compare the threshold against `rounds-to-approve-series` minus 1 (or add and price a genuine `rework-rounds-series` cost-input), and correct the "sits below the smallest ever needed" sentence in the Worked substitution and the `implement — tier2` dispatch-rule trigger accordingly.
- **The break-even inequality mixes a concurrent-resource cost with a serial wall-clock benefit on the same `(N−1) × engineer-phase-cost` term.** Tier 2 is defined as running its N candidate implementations *concurrently* ("run 2+ independent engineer attempts... concurrently"), so the real wall-clock added by the N−1 discarded attempts is close to zero — they finish alongside the winning candidate, not after it. `tier2-discard-cost`, however, prices the discard as `(N−1) × engineer-phase-cost` in the same `ms` unit the benefit side (`rework-round-cost`, a genuine calendar-time figure) uses, and sums the two directly in the inequality. That additive framing is only valid if the N−1 implementations ran *serially*, which contradicts the tier's own definition, or if `engineer-phase-cost` here actually means a resource/compute-hours quantity rather than wall-clock — in which case it should not be added directly to a wall-clock benefit term without a stated conversion. Confirmed independently by the adversarial pass ("neither elapsed-time cost nor clearly resource cost, making the thresholds optimistic and especially unreliable for N=3"). This is a genuine dimensional-consistency gap in the cost model's construction, not merely a wording nit, and it affects both worked-substitution results (N=2 and N=3), not just the N=3 case the adversarial pass called out as "especially unreliable." Fix: either state explicitly that `engineer-phase-cost` is being used as a resource/opportunity-cost proxy (not wall-clock) when multiplied by `(N−1)`, with the unit relabeled or the conversion stated, or replace the discard term with a genuine wall-clock cost (e.g., orchestration/contention overhead of running N concurrent instances, which is near-zero at small N) and price the resource cost as a separate, clearly-labelled term.
