#### Major (re-opened on the same component: the merge-unreachability derivation)
- `docs/loop-engineering/tier2-judge-briefing.md:110-117` — the reworked two-half derivation ((a) every floor-eligible candidate necessarily writes `cli/csvstats`; (b) a below-floor candidate's pieces cannot be merged into eligibility) is a genuine improvement — (a) is sound and independently confirmed (read `acceptance.sh`'s `cli_rel` resolution and all 11 `run_cli` call sites myself; matches QA's empirical "fresh venue, no `cli/csvstats` → `0 passed, 11 failed`" test) — but (a)+(b) are **not actually jointly exhaustive**, and the derivation's own closing sentence overclaims that they are. Counterexample, constructed independently and then confirmed by Codex twice (confirmation-r2 pass, unguided-r2 pass, both without being shown the other's answer): candidate **X** writes only `cli/csvstats` (nothing forbids this — none of the four enforced `- candidate-rule-map:` rules requires writing the two lib files separately) and is floor-eligible (11/0); candidate **Y** writes only `lib/colstats.sh` (never touches `cli/csvstats`) and is below-floor (0/11, by the same mechanism (a) itself proves). X and Y's written paths (`cli/csvstats` vs. `lib/colstats.sh`) are **textually disjoint** — `path-disjointness` structurally *clears* between them, so (a) does not apply to this pair (it only blocks two *floor-eligible* candidates from merging). Does (b) block it instead? Re-reading (b)'s own text: "a check that could not run is not a check that passed... means a merged tree assembled from such a candidate's pieces inherits no `check-results` of its own that the judge can score." This establishes an **absence of evidence** for the merged result — it does not establish a **rule barring the judge from proposing it**. Nothing in the region says a `merge` disposition requires the judge to already hold scored `check-results` for the *assembled* tree before selecting it (indeed `- judge-bound: phase-position` places the judge *before* Validate, implying downstream verification of the actual composed tree is expected to happen later, not at judge time) — and critically, `- selection-rule: floor` reads "a candidate is eligible for `pick` **only if**..." and "never eligible for `pick`" — it textually scopes itself to `pick` alone and says nothing about `merge`-contributor eligibility. So Y being below-floor bars Y from `pick`, but the derivation never states that it also bars Y's pieces from `merge`. X+Y survives both (a) and (b) as currently written. The derivation's own closing sentence compounds this: "a proper-subset-writing candidate that is *also* floor-eligible — cannot arise" is false as written — X *is* exactly a proper-subset-writing, floor-eligible candidate (confirmed independently and by Codex's unguided-r2 pass, flagged as a Minor in isolation, but it is symptomatic of the same Major gap: the sentence's own reasoning for why the residual case "cannot arise" does not actually follow from what (a) proves). AC4(iii)'s own substance bar ("the derivation must conclude that the branch is unreachable rather than that it is unlikely") is not met for this residual case — nothing textually unreachable-izes it, it is merely unevidenced. This is a second, independent Major landing on the same component (the AC4(iii) merge-unreachability derivation) that round 1 also found — see Pre-commitment adjudication below. Suggested fix, within this task's own authoring latitude (`- selection-rule:` lines are this document's own coined vocabulary, not verbatim-quoted from `phase-multiplexing.md`, so extending one does not touch any byte-locked source): extend `- selection-rule: floor` (or add one sentence to the derivation) to state explicitly that **every contributing candidate in a `merge` must also individually clear the floor** — this reduces the whole derivation to argument (a) alone (any two floor-eligible candidates necessarily overlap on `cli/csvstats`), closes the X+Y gap cleanly, and removes the need for (b)'s shakier evidentiary reasoning entirely.
