## Attribution and de-duplication rules

The telemetry schema (`.shell-team/runs/shell-team.jsonl`) carries **no task-id field of any kind**. Every per-task figure below rides the `run_id` naming convention alone, so the rule that convention is read by has to be stated, not assumed.

- rule: a `run_id` is **task-attributed** if it matches the ERE `(^|-)t[0-9]+$` (the dominant `<UTC-timestamp>Z-t<digits>` suffix form, e.g. `20260813T150636Z-t1065`) **or** `^t[0-9]+-` (a slug-first form, e.g. `t1061-20260812T003839Z`); the task number is the matched digit run. A `run_id` matching neither is classified `non-task-runs` rather than guessed at — measured at 6 in this corpus (`pii`, `i23`, `i24`, `i25`, `i26`, `v170`).
- rule: the **truncated form** `20260804T-t1024` (no time-of-day component after the `T`) is handled by the same suffix rule as every other `-t<digits>` `run_id` — it attributes to `T-1024` and is not treated as a parse failure or dropped.
- rule: the **slug-first form** `t1061-20260812T003839Z` is handled by the prefix rule above. A naive suffix-only pattern (matching only `-t[0-9]+$`) would misclassify this `run_id` as `non-task-runs`, which is exactly the convention break this second rule exists to catch — measured directly: `echo 't1061-20260812T003839Z' | grep -E '(^|-)t[0-9]+$'` matches nothing, `grep -E '^t[0-9]+-'` matches.
- rule: two rows sharing one `run_id` can share one `seq` value — a **duplicate seq** — while differing only in `ts` and `model`, or only in `ts`. Measured directly in the resolved telemetry file: `run_id` `20260802T052149Z-t1016` carries two rows at `seq:1` (`ts` `2026-08-02T05:24:17Z` / `2026-08-02T05:24:22Z`, `model` `null` / `"opus"`), and `run_id` `20260803T121547Z-t1022` carries two rows at `seq:1` (`ts` `2026-08-03T12:15:47Z` / `2026-08-03T12:15:57Z`, otherwise byte-identical). Any sum over span rows de-duplicates on the composite key **(`run_id`, `seq`, `span`)**, keeping the last-encountered row and discarding the earlier one; this is the key used everywhere below a sum is taken.

## Rigor vs raw latency

The numbers above bear on issue #248's own reading rule ("a rising ratio with flat tokens indicates raw latency; rising tokens/criteria with a flat ratio indicates rigor growth"), and neither a clean rigor story nor a clean latency story is what the window's own data supports.

**Rigor-side reading.** `criteria-count` and `runtime-duty-items` do not climb monotonically across the window: `criteria-count` peaks at T-1056/T-1057 (19) — the loop-liveness/loop-integration pair, both explicitly building new mechanism — and sits lower on either side (10 at T-1053, 7 at T-1062, 12 at T-1065). `freeze-versions` spikes hard at two outliers (T-1055 at v7, T-1060 at v5) against a v1–v3 baseline everywhere else in the window; both spikes correspond to tasks this repository's own project memory records as unusually contested (T-1055 the adapter-envelope contract, T-1060 adopter-facing binding docs), not to a background trend across the whole window. Read together, rigor **is not a steady climb** in this window — it is dominated by a small number of individually hard tasks, and a metric that averaged across the window would hide that rather than show it.

**Latency-side reading.** `duration-per-1k-tokens`'s by-date series (above) rises through late July into early August, peaks around `2026-08-06`–`2026-08-07` (6460.9 / 5906.7 ms per 1k tokens), then falls sharply by `2026-08-10`–`2026-08-11` (3200.3 / 1976.3) before rising again. This is the opposite of a one-directional "the executor got slower" story — it is a noisy series over 19 calendar dates from one repository's own runs, and DP6's own bound applies directly here: the numerator (`duration_ms`) and denominator (`tokens`) are both **invocation-level totals for a whole sub-agent turn** (`## Limits` below), so a week where tasks happened to ask for longer, more tool-call-heavy turns inflates the ratio's numerator and denominator together in a way this proxy cannot separate from the executor itself becoming slower per unit of work.

**What this does and does not license.** Both readings are descriptive, not statistical (this task's own Non-goals rule out any inferential claim over a 13-task, non-experimental, single-repository sample). What the data supports is: rigor growth in this window is concentrated in a few individually contested tasks rather than a smooth background climb, and the raw-latency proxy is too noisy across this window's 19 calendar dates to support a directional claim in either direction without a longer baseline and, per `## Recommended standing instrument` below, a way to hold the executor's own binding constant across the comparison.

## Limits and what is not computable

- `tokens`, `tool_uses` and `duration_ms` are read, per `skills/run/SKILL.md`, "from the `<usage>` block on the Agent tool result" for the sub-agent invocation **as a whole** — spanning that agent's own turns, tool calls and context re-reads, never a single model call. This makes `duration_ms` per 1k `tokens` an invocation-level throughput proxy and **not an ask-independent raw-latency measure**: a longer, more complex ask inflates both the numerator and the denominator together, and the ratio cannot separate "the executor is slower per unit of work" from "this particular invocation was asked to do comparatively more."
- Telemetry logging is explicitly **best-effort** (`skills/run/SKILL.md`: "if `log-run.sh` is not callable or exits non-zero, ignore it and carry on — it must never stop the loop"), so a missing row — T-1059's zero `engineer` spans and zero `codex-reviewer` spans, despite a merged spec and a review record existing for that task — is not evidence that no work happened; it is evidence only that this particular row was not logged.
- No field in the recorded schema decomposes `duration_ms` into model-inference time versus API/connection-retry time — isolating the two is unavailable in principle from this data (this task's own Input space rules it out of scope).
- `overhead-share` is declined above for the same structural reason: the schema records no orchestration/gate span with a `duration_ms` at all.
- `rounds-to-approve` for T-1058 and T-1059 is a measured floor, not a confirmed final count, because telemetry's last recorded `codex-reviewer` row for T-1058 carries verdict `REQUEST_CHANGES` (not `APPROVE`) and T-1059 carries no `codex-reviewer` row at all, even though both tasks are `READY_FOR_MERGE` in the git-tracked board and reviews corpus. This is read as a best-effort logging gap on those two tasks specifically, not as evidence those tasks never reached approval.
- No inferential-statistics claim (p-value, significance test, regression) is made anywhere in this note over this 13-task, non-experimental, single-repository sample.

## Recommended standing instrument

This task ships **no mechanism ships in this task** — no extension to `bin/rollup-runs.sh`, no new script, no CI wiring. What it recommends, for a future before/after comparison of an executor change under v2.0.0's binding config, is a metric set expressed against T-1058's own per-span binding keys — `provider`, `model`, `effort`, `adapter` — rather than against role name alone:

- Continue recording `engineer-wall-clock-total` and `duration-per-1k-tokens`, but **grouped by the (`provider`, `model`, `effort`, `adapter`) tuple** recorded on each span (per `## Population and exclusions`' positive control above, `"provider"` is already present on telemetry rows), not merely by role — a rebind of `codex-reviewer` from Codex to a same-family executor, for instance, is a change to that tuple's value for the `codex-reviewer` role, and grouping by the tuple is what would let a before/after comparison isolate the executor's own contribution from the row's own producer role.
- Hold `criteria-count`, `runtime-duty-items` and `freeze-versions` alongside the latency-side pair as the rigor-side baseline, precisely because none of the three depends on which executor ran the task — a binding change that holds the spec's own text constant should leave these three metrics unmoved, and a before/after comparison that sees them move too has mixed a rigor change into what was meant to be an executor comparison.
- Do not use `overhead-share` until the schema records a `duration_ms` for orchestration/gate transitions; recommending its future addition to the schema is itself outside this task's remit (`no mechanism ships in this task` again — that decision belongs to whoever files the mechanization request this note's `DP8` anticipates).

Whoever wants any of the above mechanized — wired into `bin/rollup-runs.sh` or a new script — files that as a separate task; this note commits to no mechanism and builds none.
